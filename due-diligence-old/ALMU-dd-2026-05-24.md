## Bonus — Technology Deep Dive (QD lasers)

### What are quantum-dot lasers and why do they matter for AI infrastructure?

Quantum-dot (QD) lasers use nano-scale semiconductor "dots" (typically InAs or InGaAs embedded in GaAs or InP host matrix) as the gain medium, in contrast to traditional quantum-well (QW) lasers that use thin 2D layers. The QD architecture provides three engineering advantages relevant to silicon photonics integration:

1. **Lower threshold current and lower temperature sensitivity** — QD lasers can operate uncooled at temperatures up to ~120°C, important for co-packaged optics where laser dies are integrated next to high-power CMOS switching ASICs.
2. **Tolerance to defects** — QD active regions are less sensitive to dislocations than QW regions, which matters when growing III-V lasers directly on silicon (the III-V/Si lattice mismatch creates dislocations that destroy QW lasers but are partially tolerated by QD lasers).
3. **Optical-isolator-free operation** — the broader QD gain bandwidth provides natural feedback insensitivity, eliminating the need for expensive Faraday-rotator isolators in some configurations.

For AI infrastructure specifically, the QD-on-silicon thesis is: NVIDIA's Spectrum-X Photonics co-packaged optics architecture requires hundreds of laser sources per switch ASIC, currently supplied as discrete InP DFB lasers wire-bonded or hybrid-integrated onto silicon photonic ICs. Direct epitaxial growth of QD lasers on 300mm silicon would eliminate the discrete-laser assembly step and dramatically reduce cost-per-port. This is a real, NVDA-acknowledged category-level chokepoint.

### IP position and university origin

Aeluma's stated IP portfolio: ~30 issued and pending US patents (per 10-K Item 1). Klamkin's UCSB Integrated Photonics Laboratory (iPL) has been publishing in this space since at least 2013-14, with subsequent work under DARPA's E-PHI (Electronic-Photonic Heterogeneous Integration, 2011-14) and LUMOS programs. The 2025 published work on "Heteroepitaxial III–V/SiNₓ Integrated Photonics (HIP)" comes out of this same academic lineage.

**Critical gap:** the 10-K Item 1 Business section does NOT disclose the UCSB-to-Aeluma technology transfer / licensing structure. Specifically:
- Is the foundational IP licensed exclusively to Aeluma, non-exclusively, or held outright by UCSB?
- What royalty/milestone obligations does Aeluma owe to UCSB?
- Does the University of California system retain march-in rights under Bayh-Dole for federally-funded research?
- Are there competing licenses (e.g., to Bowers' group at UCSB, which also works on integrated photonics on silicon)?

These questions are not answerable from the available SEC disclosures. **Recommend fetching the DEF 14A and the full 10-K Item 1A / Item 13 sections before any position reconsideration.**
