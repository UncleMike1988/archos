## Methodology note

The v2 screen runs the v1 one-degree reverse-search PLUS a two-degree
extension. Two passes per mid-chain company:

**Pass A (Forward)** — Read mid-chain's 10-K business overview and
risk-factors sections for named supplier dependencies. This pass was
expensive: only COHR's `business_overview` returned (43K chars).
LITE, AAOI, and FN accessions 404'd on `filing_section` lookup (known
EdgarTools issue per INSIGHTS.md). Worked around by reading FN/AAOI/LITE
via the company_brief summaries plus filing-language full-text search.

**Pass B (Reverse)** — `search_filings_full_text` on sub-$5B 10-Ks
naming each mid-chain. Sub-$5B verification via web search for live
market cap.

Same boolean-AND-with-phrase-quotes limitation confirmed (`NEAR(a,b,N)`
returned 0 — INSIGHTS.md note holds). Switched to single-phrase queries.

## Top names (narrative)

### 1. ICHR (Ichor Holdings) — sub-$5B two-degree WFE-chain supplier

**Chain documented:**
- NVIDIA buys chips from TSMC (bellwether, direct).
- TSMC buys WFE from Applied Materials (AMAT) and Lam Research (LRCX).
- ICHR sells gas/fluid delivery subsystems to AMAT and LRCX.
- **76% of ICHR FY2025 revenue is from those two named customers.**

**Filing passage (verbatim, [ICHR FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652535/000165253526000012/0001652535-26-000012-index.htm)):**
> "For 2025, two customers with individual sales over 10%, Lam Research
> and Applied Materials, accounted for a combined 76% of total sales.
> We do not have long-term contracts that..."

**What they supply:** Gas delivery, fluid delivery, chemical delivery
modules, frame & weldments for semiconductor wafer fabrication equipment.
Per ICHR 10-K, they are positioned in the WFE upstream supply chain —
"semiconductor wafer fabrication equipment manufacturer market is highly
concentrated with five companies accounting for over 70% of all
semiconductor wafer fabrication equipment revenues."

**Chokepoint mapping:** No direct entry in `CHOKEPOINT_TAXONOMY.md`.
The existing #4 chokepoint (wafer-level burn-in test, AEHR) is in the
same family but covers a different stage of the wafer process. ICHR
covers the wet/gas delivery side of front-end. **If a new chokepoint
entry is to be added, it would be "WFE upstream subsystems (gas, fluid,
plasma power)"** — but the conviction is lower because demand is
broadly WFE-correlated, not AI-DC specific.

**H10 conviction:** Category-level only. TSMC is the bellwether, but
TSMC doesn't name ICHR directly. AMAT and LRCX (mid-chain to TSMC)
name ICHR via the 76% concentration disclosure (in ICHR's own 10-K).

**H8 risk:** Need to verify what % of ICHR's revenue is AI-data-center
WFE end-market specifically. If WFE spending broadly tracks ICHR (auto,
IoT, memory, logic all included), the AI-DC mix may be <50% — same
critique as POWL (which passed because the AI-DC slice was sufficient
and growing).

**H5 quick indicator:** Insider sentiment unknown at this depth.
Analyst coverage exists (multiple sell-side covers WFE supply chain).
Likely NEUTRAL not IGNORED.

**H11 quick indicator:** Mid-cycle FY25 earnings show ICHR mid-recovery
from prior WFE downturn. Need full balance-sheet verification.

**Recommendation:** **WATCH — full 6-check on next monthly cadence.**
This is the first 2-degree candidate the v2 screen has surfaced.
Adjacency-not-pure-play risk is real (POWL analog). DD priority:
quantify the AI-DC WFE end-market exposure and whether ICHR has
disclosed specific AI-related WFE platform participation.

### 2. UCTT (Ultra Clean Holdings) — pair-trade / parallel candidate to ICHR

**Chain documented:**
- NVIDIA → TSMC (bellwether) → AMAT + LRCX → UCTT.
- Applied Materials + Lam Research **each individually >10% of revenue**
  for fiscal years 2025, 2024, and 2023.

**Filing passage (verbatim, [UCTT FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1275014/000162828026010744/0001628280-26-010744-index.htm)):**
> "Our two largest revenue customers in fiscal years 2025, 2024 and
> 2023 were Applied Materials, Inc. and Lam Research Corporation, each
> of which accounted for more than 10% of our total revenues in those
> three years."

**What they supply:** Outsourced subsystems (gas delivery, frames,
liquid delivery, sub-assemblies) and high-sensitivity micro
contamination analysis for semiconductor device makers and WFE markets.

**Chokepoint mapping:** Same as ICHR — WFE upstream subsystems. Not
in current taxonomy.

**H10 conviction:** Category-level. Same chain shape as ICHR.

**H8 risk:** Same critique as ICHR — broadly WFE-correlated, may not be
>50% AI-DC end-market. **Need verification.** UCTT is closer to the $5B
ceiling at $3.6-3.8B than ICHR at $2.4B; less re-rate runway.

**Recommendation:** **WATCH alongside ICHR.** If H8 verifies for one, it
likely verifies for the other given identical customer concentration
shape. If both DD-clear, the smaller (ICHR) gets priority for the
dose-response reasons in INSIGHTS.md.

## AXTI retroactive validation (MANDATORY)

### Test 1: Does AXTI's recent 10-K name COHR / LITE / EMCORE / IQE as a customer?

**Result: FAIL.**

AXTI's most recent 10-K ([filed 2026-03-17 for FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1051627/000143774926008612/0001437749-26-008612-index.htm), accession 0001437749-26-008612)
does NOT name Coherent, Lumentum, EMCORE, IQE, Broadcom, NVIDIA, or
any other bellwether or mid-chain company by name. AXTI's only
explicit disclosure is:

> "There are significant barriers to entry in the InP substrate market
> and currently there are only three primary suppliers, including AXT."

> "growth of AI applications is increasing the need for high-speed
> data transfer, which may enable a growing increase in InP substrate
> demand for such data centers. Currently, InP substrates are being
> used in certain consumer products, including proximity..."

AXTI markets to AI as a category but doesn't name any specific bellwether
or mid-chain customer.

### Test 2: Does COHR's recent 10-K name AXTI as a substrate supplier?

**Result: FAIL — but for a structural reason: COHR is VERTICALLY INTEGRATED on InP.**

COHR FY2025 10-K ([filed 2025-08-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820318/000082031825000014/0000820318-25-000014-index.htm), accession 0000820318-25-000014)
explicitly states:

> "We also have multiple InP fabs in the U.S. and Europe, and we are
> moving to 6-inch wafer capability."

> "Coherent has multiple 6-inch GaAs VCSEL fabs in the U.S. and Europe."

COHR grows its own InP and GaAs internally. It is the largest
vertically integrated optical company — its supply-chain
disclosure ("Sources of Supply" section) names generic categories
("ICs, DSPs, mechanical housings, and optical components") but no
specific named substrate vendor.

This is **a structural reason the v2 screen still cannot surface
AXTI via COHR**: COHR does not buy InP substrates from AXTI —
it makes its own.

### Test 3: Does LITE's recent 10-K name AXTI as a substrate supplier?

**Result: FAIL — same vertical-integration pattern.**

LITE's FY2025 10-K disclosure on suppliers: "packages and components
that our suppliers supply to us. We have not entered into long-term
agreements with many of these suppliers." Generic.

Industry context: LITE acquired Cloud Light in 2023 and operates its
own InP fabs (the former Oclaro / Lumentum heritage). Similar
vertical-integration pattern to COHR.

### Test 4: Does AAOI's recent 10-K name AXTI as a substrate supplier?

**Result: FAIL — third vertical-integration confirmation.**

Per AAOI investor research and 10-K disclosures, AAOI "designs and
manufactures its own Indium Phosphide (InP) laser chips in-house,
giving the company significant control over its supply chain." Recent
partnership: AAOI + Oxford Instruments for next-generation InP
optoelectronic device manufacturing (March 2026). AAOI is moving
**deeper into vertical integration**, not relying on external substrate
suppliers.

### Diagnosis: WHY v2 still fails to surface AXTI

**The v2 design assumption — that two-degree mapping catches AXTI
via COHR / LITE / AAOI — is wrong for the optical chokepoint
because the named mid-chain players (COHR, LITE, AAOI) are ALL
vertically integrated on InP.** They grow their own substrates.
They don't buy from AXTI.

AXTI's actual customers (the three-degree layer) are likely:
- Smaller InP photodiode/laser vendors not in the v2 mid-chain list
  (e.g., MACOM-MTSI, Semtech-SMTC, Marvell-MRVL, Astera Labs-ALAB,
  smaller Chinese / Taiwanese optical chip vendors)
- Consumer electronics OEMs using InP for 3D-sensing and AR
- Foundries that fab on InP wafers for third parties
- R&D / universities

This is a **structural finding for v3**: the InP substrate chokepoint
has a mid-chain that is vertically integrated, so the "supplier two
degrees below the bellwether" framing doesn't apply. The actual
extraction point is **product-line-specific** — AXTI sells to
the long tail of optical chip designers that aren't COHR / LITE /
AAOI. Those are the v3 mid-chain to search.

**Implication for v3:** add MTSI (MACOM), MRVL, ALAB, SMTC, and the
Asia-listed photonics smallcaps (need a separate 20-F search) as
"second mid-chain" to test for AXTI customer mentions.

### What v2 DID validate

The v2 screen DOES work for the WFE chokepoint — ICHR and UCTT
surfaced cleanly as sub-$5B two-degree suppliers via AMAT/LRCX/TSMC.
The pattern works when the mid-chain is NOT vertically integrated on
the upstream input. AMAT and LRCX outsource their subsystem
manufacturing; they don't make their own gas/fluid delivery modules.

So v2 catches the "outsourced-subsystem" two-degree pattern but
misses the "vertically-integrated-substrate" two-degree pattern.

## Refinement notes

### Query effectiveness per mid-chain (reverse-pass)
- **Coherent reverse-search:** 77 hits, ~5% actionable. High noise from
  "coherent" as adjective ("coherent strategy") and from references to
  COHR competitors (LITE, FN themselves).
- **Lumentum reverse-search:** 12 hits, mostly mid-chain peers. Useful
  signal/noise but small absolute volume.
- **Fabrinet reverse-search:** 6 hits, all FN itself or noise. FN is
  too specific a term to generate many false positives, but apparently
  too specific to surface 2-degree suppliers either.
- **Applied Optoelectronics reverse-search:** 1 hit (AAOI itself). The
  full name is too distinctive — no one else uses "Applied
  Optoelectronics" in their 10-K.
- **Amkor reverse-search:** 11 hits. LSCC names Amkor as an assembly
  partner (Amkor = LSCC's supplier, wrong direction). CRDO, ALAB
  surfaces are mid-chain peers. No clean two-degree.

### Disambiguation cost
- Coherent reverse: 75% noise (sister mid-chain peers + adjective use)
- Lumentum reverse: 60% noise (peers)
- Fabrinet/AAOI reverse: minimal data
- Amkor reverse: 70% noise (most named Amkor as their own supplier,
  not as a customer)

### Forward-pass vs reverse-pass
- **Forward-pass produced no actionable substrate-supplier names** for
  COHR/LITE/AAOI because all three are vertically integrated.
- **Reverse-pass produced ICHR + UCTT** via the AMAT/LRCX path — this
  is a different two-degree chain than the original AXTI test case
  intended, but a valid one.
- **The most productive single search was `"Lam Research" AND "10%"`**
  which surfaced both ICHR and UCTT plus context on the entire WFE
  upstream supply chain.

### Foreign-filer blind spot
Not exercised this run. Asian InP / GaAs substrate competitors to AXTI
(IQE on LSE, Jiangsu Nata, Yunnan Lincang) would need a 20-F or
foreign-filing pass. Flagged for next quarter.

### Tooling friction
- `filing_section` 404'd on three accessions (LITE 0001628280-25-040830,
  AAOI 0001437749-26-005875, and AXTI's 2026 filing 0001437749-26-008612)
  — known issue per INSIGHTS.md. Workaround: read full-text-search
  snippets + use company_brief financial summary.
- Boolean AND with phrase quotes still broken. `NEAR("Coherent",
  "supplier", 10)` returned 0. Single-phrase queries only.
- Web search remains the fastest backup for live market caps; EdgarTools
  XBRL still stale and unreliable for sub-$5B market caps.

### What to change for v3
1. **Add a "vertical integration audit" step** before relying on the
   two-degree chain. If mid-chain X owns its substrate / wafer fab, the
   2-degree chain is broken — search 3rd-degree or alternate
   mid-chain.
2. **Expand the mid-chain set to include MTSI, MRVL, ALAB, SMTC** for
   the optical / InP / SerDes chokepoint chain — these are the actual
   end-customers of AXTI-class substrate vendors.
3. **Run a 20-F pass** on foreign-listed mid-chain companies (Sakura
   Internet, IQE, JCET, ASE, BESI) to catch foreign supply-chain disclosures.
4. **Build a chokepoint-by-chokepoint mid-chain map** in
   CHOKEPOINT_TAXONOMY.md, listing for each chokepoint whether the
   mid-chain is vertically integrated and what the actual
   substrate / component suppliers are.
