### Question 2 — What does the Mathews/Chitty migration actually signal?

Deep research on both appointees:

**Col Kevin Mathews:**
- Full military biography. What was JATF (Joint Acquisition Task Force) Director at SOCOM? What programs did he oversee?
- When did he retire from active duty? How long between retirement and joining SPAI?
- Is there a revolving-door concern? (Some defense companies hire recently-retired officers specifically for their influence on active procurement decisions — this is legal but ethically gray and sometimes investigated)
- The performance options ($5M/$10M/$20M milestones): what is the vesting timeline? If the options vest over 4 years, Mathews is betting SPAI reaches $20M revenue by ~2030. That's a 33x growth bet from $607K FY2025.
- Has Mathews joined any other company boards? Or is SPAI his sole post-military commitment?

**Chitty (USSOCOM Acquisition Executive):**
- Full name and biography. "Acquisition Executive" at SOCOM means this person was responsible for BUYING capabilities. They know every program, every budget line, every vendor relationship.
- What specific programs did Chitty oversee at SOCOM?
- Is there overlap between Chitty's SOCOM acquisition portfolio and SPAI's product set (counter-drone, mine detection, ISR)?
- Advisory board vs board of directors — what is the governance difference? Does Chitty have voting rights?

**The thesis test:** If Mathews (JATF Director = develops requirements) and Chitty (Acquisition Executive = buys capabilities) both left SOCOM to join SPAI, they're effectively saying "we know what SOCOM needs, we know how SOCOM buys, and SPAI has what SOCOM will buy next." This is the strongest forward signal possible from the defense procurement ecosystem. BUT — the AISP DD showed that advisory board appointments can also be narrative management for capital raises. Which is this?

### Question 3 — Does the subcontractor status actually matter?

The prior DD's biggest concern was that SPAI is a subcontractor to Skydio on SRR, not a direct Army vendor. Re-evaluate:

- In the defense AI ecosystem, is being a software/AI layer on top of a hardware platform (Skydio drone) actually a WEAKER position, or is it the position with higher margins and stickier IP?
- Analog: Palantir doesn't make servers — it provides software on top of commodity hardware. That's the higher-value position. Is SPAI's AI integration layer on Skydio's drones the same pattern?
- Can SPAI's AI software run on other drone platforms, or is it Skydio-locked? If platform-agnostic, the subcontractor label is misleading — SPAI is actually a software company that happens to deploy on Skydio hardware.
- The 150+ landmine types / 1.66M Ukraine battlefield images: this dataset is SPAI's moat, not Skydio's. The hardware is replaceable; the training data is not.
- If SOCOM deploys counter-drone or ISR capabilities, do they buy the drone (from Skydio/Shield AI/etc.) separately from the AI layer (from SPAI/etc.)? Or is it bundled? The acquisition structure determines whether SPAI is a subcontractor or an independent vendor.
