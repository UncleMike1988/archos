# Patterns & Traps

Plain-English reference distilled from the 65 real 500%+ winners in
`research/pattern-discovery/WINNER_UNIVERSE.md`. These are observations to inform
judgment — NOT a checklist, NOT a scoring system. No real winner had all of these;
they had some. The flaws were part of the entry.

## Setups that recurred among the winners

**Small, ignored, and real, just before a catalyst.** The classic moonshot: a
sub-$1-2B company the market had stopped paying attention to, with a real business,
that then got a catalyst — a big customer, a major contract, an earnings inflection,
a marquee partner naming them. Ignored + real + catalyst was the most common shape
(AXTI, AEHR, NVTS, POWL, AAOI, BE before they ran).

**A big customer or authority names them.** Many winners turned when a major player
validated them — a hyperscaler, NVIDIA, or for defense/nuclear/space names, the
government (DoD, DOE, NASA, NRO). The validation from a serious buyer was often the
spark. (For defense/nuclear/space, the government IS the "big customer" tell.)

**Fresh starts.** Spinoffs, post-bankruptcy emergences, and major restructurings
that reset the company to a clean, focused story — often with new management — tended
to re-rate hard when a catalyst hit the new entity (SNDK spinoff, WOLF emergence,
NBIS carve-out, BE refi + new CFO). New CFO/CEO at the reset was a recurring quiet tell.

**Beaten-down survivors that pivot.** A company whose old story died (failed trial,
broken IPO, sector collapse), trading for cheap, that in-licenses a new asset or
pivots — then a second catalyst surprises a market that had written it off. Common in
biotech and busted micro-caps (RLMD, POET, NVTS). The cheap dollar price makes the
recovery math easy.

**De-rated quality (the Bucket 3 shape).** Bigger, real, profitable companies that
sold off on a fear that didn't play out while the fundamentals kept compounding. Lower
volatility, more reliable 2-3x than a moonshot, and the place LEAPS actually work.

**Short interest as an amplifier (not a thesis).** Several winners had high short
interest going in, which turned a fundamental move into a bigger one as shorts covered.
Useful as a "this could run harder" note on a name that already passes on its own merits
— never a reason to buy on its own.

## A note on options / LEAPS

LEAPS only work where the chain is actually affordable and liquid. Most sub-$1B and
foreign-listed names have no usable options — those are equity-only. Bucket 3 (and some
Bucket 2) is where LEAPS math tends to clear. Always check the real chain (open interest,
spread) before assuming a LEAPS play exists.

## Traps — the red flags of a promotion or fraud

These are the patterns of a manufactured or hollow story. The job here is to tell a real
company from a marketing campaign dressed as one.

**Paid promotion infrastructure.** The story is carried by paid IR / stock-promotion
outfits rather than real coverage or real capital. Watch for: IBN / InvestorBrandNetwork,
RedChip, MZ Group, Litchfield Hills, DefenseWireNews / wire-syndication networks,
"the company may receive compensation for placement" disclosures, and bot-driven cashtag
spam. If you pull the thread and the entire bull case traces back to paid placement, it's
a promotion. (Calibration cases: SPAI, PPSI, SHAZ — the story was bought.)

**Hollow substance under the hype.** Tiny or no real revenue, customers that are only
LOIs/MOUs rather than paying, a "product" that's mostly a deck. Loud narrative, thin reality.

**Toxic balance sheet.** Survives only on constant dilution — death-spiral converts,
variable-priced notes, serial ATM raises. The financing structure is bleeding shareholders.

**Promoter management.** Operators with a history of narrative-hopping from one hot theme
to the next, prior pump-and-dumps, or self-dealing. Check whether the team built anything real.

**Marketing-language adjacency.** The company NAMES a bellwether (e.g. "we use NVIDIA chips,"
"same supply chain as [big company]") rather than the bellwether naming them. Anyone can claim
adjacency; it's the reverse direction (a real buyer naming the company) that matters.

**The structural squeeze toy.** Tiny float (often via 80%+ insider ownership) + enormous
borrow fee + a forward stock split + zero real business. The move is pure microstructure, not
investable — being on the wrong side of the unwind is catastrophic (calibration case: RGC).

## The bottom line

Real company with weaknesses = a candidate to analyze and weigh.
Hollow company with a bought story = an automatic no.
Telling those two apart is the one thing Archos Equities must get right; everything else is judgment
the operator makes.
