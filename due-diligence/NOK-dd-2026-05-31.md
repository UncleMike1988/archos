## 1. Is it real? — Decisive pass. The fraud gate is a non-event; the entire job is layer-two DD.

Nokia is a **160-year-old Finnish company**, **€19.9B FY2025 net sales**, **~86,000 employees**,
audited (Deloitte), dual-listed (Nasdaq Helsinki primary, NYSE sponsored ADR), with de-facto-standard
positions in telecom RAN, IP routing, optical transport, and one of the most valuable cellular
**standard-essential patent** portfolios in the world (>26,000 patent families, >6,000 declared
5G-essential). It is **net cash (~€3B)**, pays a dividend, generated **€1.47B free cash flow** in
FY2025, and is **~2.9%-owned by NVIDIA** (a $1B October-2025 equity investment — see §5). The
"is this a promotion?" gate that exists for nano-caps is irrelevant: no paid-IR footprint, no toxic
financing, no narrative-hopping operators, full sell-side coverage, and broad institutional ownership
(top US 13F holders FMR/Fidelity 222M shares and NVIDIA 166M shares as of 3/31/26). ADR mechanics are
clean and standard (1:1, Citibank depositary). Governance is ordinary for a large European issuer
(single share class, one-vote-per-share, Finnish Companies-Act board). **Nothing trips the one hard
rule.** Everything below is fundamental DD on a real, large, profitable company in the middle of a
genuine pivot.

## 2. Which bucket? — Sits in the Bucket-3 size band, but it is NOT a de-rate. Call it what it is: a large-cap transformation/re-rating play (closest book analog: NBIS's "fits no bucket cleanly," but real-profitable and net-cash).

The operator brief tags this "Bucket 3 de-rated quality / transformation." Be precise, because the
distinction drives the whole entry decision:

- **Bucket 3, as defined in the charter, is a *de-rated* compounder** — a quality name that *pulled
  back* on a fear that didn't materialize while fundamentals kept climbing (the $NOW/ADBE shape: down
  ~40%, trough multiple, records printing). **Nokia is the opposite of that.** It did not pull back —
  it **tripled**. It does not trade at a trough multiple — it trades at a **premium** to its telecom
  peers (§10). The fear isn't being priced *in*; optimism is being priced *back*.
- What Nokia actually is: a **transformation / re-rating play at large-cap scale**. The bull case is
  not "the de-rate unwinds back to fair"; it's "the *re-rate continues* — from telecom multiples
  toward AI-networking multiples — as Optical/IP compounds and AI/data-center revenue scales." That is
  a Bucket-2-style catalyst-grower thesis wearing a $85B-cap suit, with the legacy mobile business as
  ballast.

This matters because the charter is explicit that "*a recent run is not, by itself, a reason to
pass*" — momentum-with-runway is a feature, and the 162% move may be **the thesis confirming** (NVIDIA
buying at $6, Optical inflecting +20%, the cash cow stabilizing). **But the charter is equally
explicit that "it ran" ≠ "it's expensive," and that genuine overvaluation is a real finding for the
bear case.** Nokia is the case where you have to hold both: the catalyst is real and possibly early,
*and* the price already embeds a meaningful slice of the re-rate. The closest analog in the book is
**NBIS** (momentum hyper-growth, "fits no bucket cleanly," quality-yes/entry-no) — except Nokia is
**genuinely profitable, net-cash, and FCF-positive**, which makes its downside far better cushioned
than NBIS's.

## 3. Bull / base / bear — equity return shape (LEAPS overlay in §14)

Anchor: FY2026 guide is **€2.0–2.5B comparable operating profit** (tracking above the midpoint after
Q1); the Nov-2025 CMD set a **2028 target of €2.7–3.2B** comparable operating profit and a **Network
Infrastructure 2025–28 net-sales CAGR of 6–8%** (with Optical + IP Networks guided to **10–12%**
long-term and **18–20% near-term**). Current ~$14.84 ≈ **~32x forward EPS (~$0.46) / ~3.6–3.8x
sales**. Telecom-peer floor (Ericsson) ≈ **1.6x sales / ~15x earnings**; AI-networking ceiling
(Arista) ≈ **>20x sales / ~42x earnings**; Ciena ≈ **48–77x forward earnings**.

**Bull (~$24–32, ~+60–115%; the telecom→AI-networking re-rate *continues*).** Optical + IP keep
compounding ~18–20%, the €1B/quarter of AI-and-cloud firm orders converts on schedule, AI/cloud climbs
from ~8% toward the high teens/20% of revenue, Mobile Infrastructure stabilizes rather than bleeds,
and op profit marches to the €2.7–3.2B 2028 target. The market stops pricing Nokia as "Ericsson-plus"
and gives it a *discounted* AI-infrastructure multiple — say **5–8x sales** (still far below ANET's
20x) — worth roughly **$130–175B EV ≈ $24–32/share**. The NVIDIA stake and the May insider buying turn
out to be the early tell, and a 6G/AI-RAN cycle (NVIDIA partnership, T-Mobile trials) layers on. This
is a *2–2.5x*, and it requires the multiple *and* the earnings to both keep working — not just one.

**Base (~$17–21, ~+15–40%; the re-rate half-continues).** Nokia hits the €2.0–2.5B 2026 guide,
Optical carries while Mobile is flattish, AI/cloud grows but stays a minority of revenue, and the
multiple holds at today's premium-telecom/early-AI level rather than expanding much. You compound with
earnings and a modest multiple drift, landing roughly where the *freshest* AI-aware analyst targets
already sit (Morgan Stanley $16.50 "top pick," Argus/CFRA $15–16, JPMorgan €12) — i.e., the run
continues at a slower grade. This is the "guidance is met, story stays intact, nobody re-rates it to
Arista" outcome.

**Bear (~$8–11, ~-25–45%; the multiple mean-reverts toward telecom).** AI/data-center demand proves
smaller, lumpier, or more competed than the order book implies (Ciena/Arista/Cisco push back; "wins"
stay unnamed or self-funded); Mobile Infrastructure — still **~60% of revenue** — keeps shrinking and
drags blended growth into the low single digits; and the market concludes this is a telecom-equipment
maker with an optical kicker, not an AI-networking platform. At that point a ~32x forward / ~3.7x
sales multiple is indefensible and it mean-reverts toward the **Ericsson floor (~1.6x sales / ~15x
earnings)**, which is **below today's price** — the 162% run partially unwinds. The downside is
*cushioned* by net cash, ~€1.5B of 70%-margin patent royalties, and FCF — but it is real, and it is
larger than for a name that hadn't already tripled.

**Shape:** roughly *symmetric-to-slightly-favorable*, but the variance here is **both multiple and
fundamentals** (unlike ADBE, where it was almost purely multiple). You are not buying a record-setting
franchise at a decade-low multiple; you are buying a real transformation, already partly priced, where
the bull needs two engines (re-rate + earnings inflection) to keep firing and the bear is a multiple
de-rate from an elevated base.

## 4. The AI pivot — how real? Real and inflecting at the *growth edge*, but still ~8% of revenue, and the splashiest "wins" are old, unnamed, or partly self-funded.

This is the load-bearing question, and the verified picture is genuinely encouraging *and* needs the
hype trimmed:

**What's solidly real (the bull's evidence):**
- **Optical Networks grew +20% YoY in Q1-2026** (verified), and management explicitly attributes it to
  **AI/hyperscaler demand + Infinera synergies**, not traditional telecom. CEO Hotard: *"we already see
  this demand in AI factories, both in data-center interconnect and inside the data center in routing
  and switching."*
- **~€1B of new AI-and-cloud firm purchase orders in Q1 alone**, described as backlog with **12–18-month
  lead times** in optical — i.e., booked, not pipeline. AI-and-cloud customer sales **+49% YoY**.
- The **Infinera acquisition is closed and is the spine of the optical story** (§4a). Post-Infinera,
  Nokia is the **#2 optical-systems vendor globally (~15–20% share)** behind Huawei and roughly level
  with Ciena — a genuine step-up in a market that grew to **~$16B in 2025 (+15% YoY)** on hyperscaler/
  neocloud demand.
- Management *raised* the Network Infrastructure 2026 growth guide to **12–14%** and the Optical + IP
  near-term guide to **18–20%** — guidance moving up, not down.

**What the brief overstates — correct these in your head:**
- **AI/data-center is ~8% of total group revenue today.** The thesis is "8% growing 49% becomes a
  visible second engine," not "the engine is already here." Reaching the brief's "**30%+** of revenue"
  is **not a Nokia-disclosed target** — it is an extrapolation. It would require AI/cloud to roughly
  quadruple as a revenue share while telecom stays flat/shrinks — directionally plausible given the
  order book, but it's *your* assumption, not management's number.
- **Named data-center design wins are thin.** The genuinely *buyer-named* win is **Microsoft Azure** —
  but that is a **2022** data-center *switching* deal (Nokia 7250 IXR running open-source SONiC), not
  fresh 2026 AI-optical news; don't date it to the pivot. **Nscale** is named (Nokia is the networking
  partner for Nscale's build, tied to OpenAI's "Stargate Europe") — **but Nokia is also a Series B
  *investor* in Nscale**, so it's partly self-funded validation, not pure arm's-length buying. The
  Q1-2026 "several important AI & Cloud design wins" management cited have **no named logos** — treat
  as company-side language until a customer confirms.
- The **Sunnyvale "AI Networking Innovation Lab"** (launched May 21, 2026) is real, but its named
  partners are **AMD, Lenovo, Supermicro, Keysight, Weka, VIAVI, Nscale, Everpure — NOT NVIDIA.** The
  brief conflates the lab with the NVIDIA relationship; NVIDIA is a separate equity investor/strategic
  partner (§5), not a named lab partner.

**Honest synthesis.** The AI/data-center pivot is **a real, management-credited, order-backed inflection
at the margin** — the +20% optical and €1B firm orders are the strongest single pillar in the whole
file. But it is **early and small** (~8% of revenue), the marquee logos are mostly undisclosed, and one
flagship (Nscale) is partly Nokia-funded. The pivot is a *direction with real momentum*, not yet a
*destination*.

### 4a. The Infinera acquisition — verified, and it is the optical engine.
- **Announced June 27, 2024; closed February 28, 2025.** ~**$2.3B** equity value, **$6.65/share**, cash/
  stock election. Increased Nokia's optical scale by ~**75%**.
- Brings **DWDM optical systems, ICE coherent optical engines** (ICE-X coherent pluggables; ICE-D
  intra-data-center optics), and — strategically — **vertically-integrated InP photonic-integrated-circuit
  (PIC) fabrication**: an in-house **InP fab in Sunnyvale, CA** plus advanced **test/packaging in
  Allentown & Bethlehem, PA** (Bethlehem secured ~$93M of federal CHIPS-style funding). Owning the
  photonics supply chain is a real edge as coherent optics move into the data center. *(Brief
  imprecision: it implied a generic "US InP fab"; the fab is Sunnyvale, the PA sites are packaging/test.)*
- **David Heard, the former Infinera CEO, is now President of Nokia's Network Infrastructure** (effective
  July 1, 2025; Group Leadership Team; Dallas-based) — the optical/AI-DC franchise is run by the man who
  built Infinera.

### 4b. Competitive set.
- **Optical (Nokia's real edge):** #2 globally post-Infinera; **Ciena (CIEN)** is the key Western rival
  and dominant in North America (~50% US share) on WaveLogic 6e and 400ZR/ZR+. Huawei #1 globally.
- **IP/switching (Nokia is a challenger, not a leader):** **Arista (ANET)** owns the hyperscaler back-end
  Ethernet fabric; **Cisco (CSCO)** is the incumbent; **Juniper was absorbed by HPE** (DOJ settlement
  June 28, 2025; deal closed **July 2, 2025**), consolidating the enterprise/AI-DC routing field. Nokia's
  data-center switching is a **distant follower** leaning on SONiC + the Microsoft/Nscale wins — its edge
  is optical, not switching.

## 7. The reorganization — three segments effective Jan 1, 2026; Portfolio Businesses is real; **Fixed Networks fiber is NOT for sale** (brief wrong).

Announced at the **November 20, 2025 Capital Markets Day** (Hotard's first), effective **January 1, 2026**,
first reported in Q1-2026 — **three operating segments + Group Common & Other:**

1. **Network Infrastructure** (the growth engine; President **David Heard**): **Optical Networks + IP
   Networks + Fixed Networks (fiber/broadband)**. Guided **12–14% growth for 2026**.
2. **Mobile Infrastructure** (the cash cow; run by **Hotard on an interim basis**): **Radio Networks +
   Core Software + Technology Standards** (the IP-licensing annuity, formerly "Nokia Technologies"). Mature
   5G/6G telecom equipment — cash-generative but flat-to-declining.
3. **Portfolio Businesses** (the divestiture bucket): the sub-scale, dilutive tail Nokia will "decide on
   during 2026."

**Portfolio Businesses — four units (brief missed one):** **(1) Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) CPE, (2)
Site Implementation & Outside Plant, (3) Enterprise Campus Edge, and (4) Microwave Radio** — combined
~**€0.9B net sales** at an ~**€0.1B operating *loss***. Selling these is *cleanup*, not a crown-jewel
sale. **FWA CPE is already sold:** on **April 30, 2026** Nokia agreed to divest it to **Inseego (INSG)** —
Nokia takes a ~7%→~11% Inseego equity stake (~$20M) + a $10M cash investment for the ~$200M-revenue unit;
closes Q4 2026; not financially material.

**Correction the operator should note:** the brief says "**Fixed Networks (fiber/broadband) is being
evaluated for potential sale.**" **That is wrong.** Fixed Networks (fiber/IP/optical) is **core and
retained inside Network Infrastructure** and is being *invested in* (fiber OLT is called a growth pipeline;
Optical + IP guides were *raised*). Only the *sub-units* (FWA CPE, Site Implementation) were carved out
into Portfolio Businesses. The brief appears to have conflated the FWA CPE carve-out with the whole fiber
business. **"Remaining Nokia" after the Portfolio sales** is cleaner: two growth/cash engines (Network
Infrastructure + Mobile Infrastructure) shedding a ~€0.9B / €0.1B-loss tail — supportive of the €2.7–3.2B
2028 op-profit target.

## 10. Valuation & comps — the crux, and the reason this is *not* a layup: Nokia is **not cheap**.

**The honest read on price:**
- At ~$14.84 Nokia trades at **~32x forward earnings and ~3.6–3.8x sales** — *above* pure-telecom
  Ericsson (1.6x sales / ~15x) and at a clear *premium*, not a discount. The brief's "~3.8x EV/sales" is
  **correct at the current price** (an earlier aggregator snapshot showing ~2.9x used a stale ~$70B cap;
  at $85B cap / ~$82B EV on FY2025 net sales the ratio is ~3.6–3.8x).
- These multiples look rich because the **earnings base is depressed/transitioning** (comparable op margin
  only ~10%; FY2025 comparable op profit €2.0B), not necessarily because the price is absurd — but make no
  mistake, **this is not a cheap stock.** Unlike ADBE (11x forward, records printing), Nokia has *already*
  been partly re-rated, and the bull case requires the multiple to keep expanding **and** earnings to
  inflect.

**The misclassification math (the actual thesis):**
- **Telecom floor (ERIC, ~1.6x sales):** ~$34B EV ≈ **below today's price** → that's the bear's gravity.
- **Premium-telecom / early-AI (~4–5x sales):** ~$88–110B EV ≈ **$16–20/share.**
- **Discounted AI-networking (~6–8x sales, still far under ANET's 20x):** ~$130–175B EV ≈ **$24–32/share**
  → the bull's $24–32.

So the re-rate, *if it happens*, is worth a 1.6–2.2x from here; a full Arista-style 20x-sales re-rate is
**fantasy** for a company with Nokia's single-digit blended growth and ~10% margins. **The realistic bull
is a *partial* re-rate from "Ericsson-plus" toward "credible AI-infrastructure participant," and the bear
is mean-reversion toward the Ericsson floor.** That two-sidedness — and the fact that you're entering
*after* a 162% run at a premium multiple — is why "quality yes, but mind the entry" is the honest verdict.

**Analysts:** consensus **Buy/Strong Buy** (TipRanks 6 Buy / 2 Hold / 0 Sell), but **price targets are
noisy and lagging the run** — the *average* (~$13) is already below the price because half the panel hasn't
refreshed since the stock doubled. The **freshest, AI-aware** targets cluster **$15–17.50**: Morgan Stanley
$16.50 ("top pick," Overweight), Argus Buy $15, CFRA Buy $16, JPMorgan €12 Overweight. Net: the Street sees
modest further upside but **most analysts have not underwritten a full AI re-rate** — there's room for
estimates to chase the story up *or* for disappointment to bite.

## 11. Technology Standards (IP licensing) — the quiet, high-quality annuity; the "$10–15B patents" tag is a *derived* SOTP, not a market mark.

- **~€1.5B FY2025 net sales at a >70% operating margin** — ~6% of revenue but a disproportionate share of
  profit, and the **highest-quality earnings stream in the company** (the brief's "€1.1–1.4B" is slightly
  low). **>€800M/yr of recurring contracted royalties locked through 2030** (smartphone renewals) plus
  >€200M/yr from expansion areas (auto, IoT, multimedia/video). Portfolio: **>26,000 patent families,
  >6,000 declared 5G-essential**, most with 10+ years of life.
- **Recent deals (real, paying, litigation-resolving):** Amazon (Mar-2025, streaming video), Samsung
  (renewed — 5G then video-in-TVs), Hisense (Jan-2026), HP, and automotive progress (Mercedes + four
  Chinese OEMs).
- **On the "$10–15B patent value" claim:** *credible as a derived sum-of-parts*, **not** a fresh published
  analyst mark. Capitalizing the ~€1.5B/70%-margin annuity (≈€1.05B op profit) at a conservative ~10–12x
  yields **~€10–13B (~$11–14B)** — so the range is defensible as a standalone valuation of the licensing
  business, but treat it as a SOTP *input*, not a number the market is quoting. It's a real reason the
  bear's downside is cushioned: a chunk of EV is a contracted, high-margin royalty stream independent of
  the AI story.

  roll-up logic) — a further optical/DC bolt-on would be on-thesis.

## 13. The "already ran 162%" question — is there a second leg, and is it starting?

The charter says a 2–3x run can be the thesis *confirming*, and Nokia is a fair candidate for that read:
- **What drove the first leg:** Hotard's pivot narrative + the Infinera close + **Optical +20%** and the
  Optical/IP guide lifted from 10–12% to 18–20% + **AI/cloud +49% (now ~8%)** + the **NVIDIA $1B stake at
  $6** + the Nscale/Stargate-Europe networking role + the Sunnyvale lab. That is a *fundamental* re-rating
  off a ~$4 trough, not a momentum squeeze (short interest is not the story here).
- **What a second leg needs:** AI/cloud to keep compounding from 8% toward the high-teens, Mobile
  Infrastructure to stabilize, the €2.0–2.5B (then €2.7–3.2B) op-profit path to print, and the multiple to
  drift from "Ericsson-plus" toward "AI-infrastructure participant." The **May insider buying at $15–16** by
  Owczarek and Hanrahan, and NVIDIA's locked-in 2.9%, are the real-time tells that informed insiders think
  the second leg is live — *paying up into the run rather than waiting.*
- **The counter:** a lot of that is already in a ~32x-forward / ~3.7x-sales multiple. The second leg is **not
  free** — you're paying for optionality on AI/DC that is still 8% of revenue. The asymmetry is far less
  favorable than ADBE's was (where you paid 11x for records); here the price and the setup only *partly*
  agree.

## What kills it — the one or two things that break the thesis

**1. The AI/data-center engine stays too small while the multiple is too big.** AI/cloud is ~8% of revenue;
if its growth proves lumpier or more competed than the €1B order book implies (Ciena/Arista/Cisco push
back; "wins" stay unnamed or self-funded like Nscale), and **Mobile Infrastructure — ~60% of revenue —
keeps shrinking**, then *blended* growth stays low-single-digit and the market refuses to re-rate Nokia off
the telecom complex. At ~32x forward / ~3.7x sales, a name that turns out to be "telecom with an optical
kicker" mean-reverts toward the **Ericsson floor (~1.6x sales / ~15x)** — which is **below today's price.**
This is the thesis-breaker, it's the reason "not cheap" matters, and it cannot be dismissed — only
out-printed by the optical/AI-cloud numbers (which so far are inflecting up). **Watch:** AI/cloud as a % of
revenue and the firm-order conversion; Optical/IP growth holding ~18–20%; Mobile Infrastructure
stabilizing; named (not anonymous) hyperscaler wins.

**2. Execution/transition risk on a brand-new operating model.** A first-year CEO, a January-2026 reorg, an
Infinera integration, a "Mobile Infrastructure" run interim by the CEO, and a Portfolio-Businesses
divestiture program all in flight at once. A stumble — integration friction, a margin air-pocket in the
mobile cash cow, a Portfolio sale at a bad price, or a guidance reset — would puncture the premium multiple
quickly given how much optimism is already in the tape.

*(Secondary, lower-probability: the AI-RAN/NVIDIA-6G cycle slips to the right; FX (EUR/USD) swings the ADR;
a large lumpy patent-renewal gap dents Technology Standards optics — none thesis-defining.)*

## The crux — a real, NVIDIA-validated transformation that is *already partly priced*; the opposite of ADBE, where the price was the friend

Everything attractive about Nokia is genuine: a **160-year-old, net-cash, FCF-positive, patent-rich** real
company in the middle of a **credible AI-data-center pivot** — **Optical +20%, €1B/quarter of firm AI orders,
#2 global optical share post-Infinera, a $1B NVIDIA equity stake at $6, restored dividend, two GLT insiders
buying ~$1.8M at $15–16.** The fraud gate is a non-event and the catalyst is real and arguably early. **But
the honest, charter-mandated second look says the price has already done a lot of the work.** Unlike the
classic Bucket-3 de-rate (ADBE at 11x forward with records printing, where the price *agreed* with the
setup), Nokia has **already run 162%** and trades at a **premium telecom-to-early-AI multiple (~32x forward
/ ~3.6–3.8x sales / ~24–25x EV/EBITDA)** — *richer* than Ericsson, not cheaper. The bull case needs **two
engines firing at once** (continued re-rate **and** an earnings inflection) for the $24–32 outcome; the bear
case is a single-engine failure (AI stays 8%, Mobile drags, multiple mean-reverts toward the Ericsson floor)
that takes it **below today's price.** And the insider signal — the thing the operator cares most about — is
**real but ~$1.8M not $3.2M, two people not four-plus, with the brief's David Heard "loading up at $10–12"
claim flatly wrong (he *sold* 275k shares in February).**

**Net:** a high-quality, real, well-validated transformation with a legitimate second-leg case — but you are
buying it *after* the re-rate started, at a non-cheap multiple, on an AI engine that is still only 8% of
revenue. **Quality YES; "is it real" YES; the open questions are (a) does AI/DC scale fast enough to out-run
a shrinking mobile cash cow, and (b) is ~32x forward a sane price for that bet — which makes this a
*momentum-with-runway* name to size on a pullback or on hard proof (named hyperscaler wins, AI/cloud
crossing into the teens), not a value entry.** Closest book analog is NBIS (momentum, "fits no bucket
cleanly," quality-yes/entry-watch) — but Nokia is profitable, net-cash, and far better cushioned, so the
downside is real but not catastrophic.
