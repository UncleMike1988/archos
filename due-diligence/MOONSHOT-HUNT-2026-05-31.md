## 1. Is it real? — Clean pass. A 40-year-old listed semicap maker, not a promotion.

The fraud gate is over in a sentence. Amtech is a **Nasdaq-listed company since the 1980s**,
~$79M of audited FY2025 revenue, two operating subsidiaries with physical manufacturing (BTU
International in North Billerica, MA; PR Hoffman in Carlisle, PA; plus operations in China,
Canada and Singapore), real OSAT/OEM customers, KPMG-grade audited financials, and an
institutional-but-thin register anchored by **Kokino LLC** (a value-oriented family office,

paid-IR footprint** (no IBN/RedChip/MZ Group), no cashtag-bot spam, no toxic converts, no
death-spiral financing. The bellwether attribution runs the right direction — the demand is
*buyer-pull* ("AI product demand drove the 31% growth"), not Amtech claiming adjacency.
**Nothing trips the promotion/fraud test. Move on.**

The honest asterisk: "real" here also means **real-and-small-and-historically-cyclical.** This
is a company whose operating income has whipsawed (−$0.5M → +$3.7M → +$17.3M → −$15.0M →
−$6.7M → −$28.5M across FY2020–25) and whose revenue fell 22% in FY2025. It is not a serene
compounder; it is a sub-scale semicap name that lives and dies on the equipment cycle and on
landing the right high-value applications. The fraud gate is the wrong place to dwell — the
real DD is whether the AI-advanced-packaging mix is finally the durable, margin-accretive
driver that re-rates it, and everything below serves that question.

## 2. Which bucket? — Bucket 1 moonshot. Sub-$500M, equity-only, 10x-shaped torque.

At ~$306M it is squarely Bucket-1 by size (sub-$1B nano/microcap), and Bucket-1 by *character*:

## 3. The chokepoint — AI advanced packaging, and ASYS genuinely sits on it. (Verified.)

The binding constraint in AI silicon has migrated from the wafer fab into the **back-end /
advanced-packaging** node: CoWoS-class 2.5-D integration, HBM stacking, chiplet/substrate
assembly — the steps where the GPU, the HBM stacks and the interposer are joined into a
package. TSMC's CoWoS capacity has been the single most-cited bottleneck of the cycle, and the
OSATs (ASE, Amkor, SPIL, Powertech) plus the IDMs are racing to add advanced-packaging
capacity. **Reflow/thermal processing is a required step in that flow** — solder-bump reflow,
flip-chip attach, thermal-compression-adjacent processes, and the curing/baking steps on thin
substrates and large interposers.

ASYS's TPS segment (BTU International) makes exactly that equipment, and the public record is
specific, not hand-wavy: BTU's reflow systems are described as used **"by leading OSATs and
OEMs for advanced packaging of most AI semiconductor devices,"** and BTU's **TrueFlat reflow
oven** is purpose-built to process **very thin substrates with exceptional flatness and
uniformity** — the precise pain point in warpage-prone large AI packages and thin-core
substrates. Management's Q2 FY26 language is direct: **"AI product demand drove the 31% growth
in net revenues"; AI-related revenue is "over 30% of Thermal Processing Solutions segment
sales"** with **"very strong" bookings**, and **AI-related TPS equipment is anticipated to
account for as much as 40% of segment sales in fiscal Q3 2026.** This is a real, shipping,
buyer-pulled position on a verified AI chokepoint — the strongest single element of the thesis.

**The honest governor:** reflow is a *real* step but a *more contested* one than AEHR's
wafer-level burn-in. BTU competes against Heller Industries, Rehm, Kurtz Ersa and Asian
suppliers; reflow ovens are a broader SMT-assembly product category that BTU is pushing
up-market into advanced packaging. So the position is "differentiated product winning a growing
high-end slice," not "sole-source monopoly." The advanced-packaging *application* is the
high-value, defensible end; the question (Section 9) is how big and how durable ASYS's share of
it becomes.

## 5. Margins, profitability and the clean-out year — the FY2025 loss was mostly non-cash.

  loss in a trough year, *not* a $30M cash bleed. Tellingly, goodwill is now down to **$0.9M**

  the impairment risk is largely *spent*, not looming.
- **Operating cash flow was +$7.9M in FY2025** even through the bad year, and capex is light —

- **The recent quarters are profitable and improving:** Q1 FY26 net income +$108K; **Q2 FY26

So the profitability arc is: ugly impairment trough (FY2025) → back to GAAP black and expanding
margins (H1 FY26) on the AI mix. The Adjusted-EBITDA margin (~12%) is still thin and the

## 9. Competition & moat — the central "how durable" question, stated plainly.

**For the bull:** BTU has decades in thermal processing, a purpose-built thin-substrate product
(TrueFlat) aimed at the exact warpage problem in large AI packages, an installed base at leading
OSATs/OEMs, and a margin profile (47.7% GM) that says the AI mix is genuinely higher-value.
**Against the bull:** reflow is a more contested category than wafer-level burn-in. BTU competes
with **Heller Industries, Rehm Thermal Systems, Kurtz Ersa**, and lower-cost Asian suppliers;
the OSATs are sophisticated buyers who dual-source equipment; and "advanced packaging" reflow is
adjacent to a broad SMT-assembly oven market where price competition is real. The PR Hoffman
(consumables) segment is steadier but lower-growth and also competitive (lapping/polishing
carriers and CMP consumables are a fragmented market).

**Net:** the moat is **"differentiated product taking share in a structurally growing high-end,"
not a monopoly.** That caps the *certainty* of the upside (this is not AEHR's near-sole-source
SiC burn-in position) but does not negate it — share gains in advanced-packaging reflow on a
$79M revenue base can still double or triple the company, and the AI-packaging TAM is expanding
