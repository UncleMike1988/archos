gated behind execution and one EPA stamp.*

## The bird-dog read (lead with why this could run)

Strip it to the interesting part first. A sub-$1, ~$50M company has, in the last three months, gone from
"shrinking oil minnow" to a **funded, FID'd, contracted** developer of a real industrial-gas asset:

- A **5-year, 100% take-or-pay helium offtake at a fixed $285/MCF** with an **investment-grade global
  industrial-gas major** — signed, not an LOI. That's the reverse-direction validation the charter prizes:
  a serious buyer committing to *them*.
- A **Ryder Scott** resource report behind it — **~1.3 Bcf net helium and ~444 Bcf net CO₂**, >50-yr life.
- The CO₂ is the quiet prize: management pegs **~$130M of Section 45Q carbon-credit value over the first 12
  years of Phase 1 alone**, gated on an **EPA MRV approval anticipated summer 2026** — a dated, binary,
  near-term catalyst on a company worth ~$50M.
- A **clean, non-toxic** balance sheet — the financing is ordinary senior-secured bank debt, not death-spiral
  convertibles — and management says Phase 1 is fully funded to first production (Q1-2027) without another
  equity raise.

That is a genuinely asymmetric setup *if* it executes. Now the hard look — and the hard look finds real
problems that don't break the fraud gate but do define the bet.

## 1. Is it real? — Yes, it passes the fraud gate. With two honest yellow flags.

This is a real, **24-year-listed (since 2001)** Delaware operating company (CIK 101594), not a shell with a
bought story. The substance is there: ~144,000 Montana acres, a **named** EPC contractor, a **signed**
investment-grade offtake, an FID with capital already deploying ($4.4M of project capex spent in Q1-2026),
a **named third-party** (Ryder Scott) resource report, and legacy oil production throwing off ~$1.6M/quarter.
Customers and contracts, not vapor.

- **Operators — real energy people, not promoters.** CEO **Ryan Smith** (43) has been with USEG since 2017
  (CFO, then CEO from Dec 2019); prior CFO of Emerald Oil and an energy banker at Canaccord. Chairman **John
  Weinzierl** co-founded and ran **Memorial Resource Development** (took two entities public). Both have an
  oil-bust bankruptcy in their wakes (Emerald Ch. 11 2016; Memorial Production Partners Ch. 11 2017) — vintage
  2016-17 shale-crash casualties, disclosed in their own bios, **not** a pump-and-dump pattern. CFO Mark Zajac
  since 2023. These are sector operators, not narrative-hoppers.
- **Promotion check — yellow, not red.** USEG *does* carry paid-IR infrastructure: a **RedChip** relationship
  and an **issuer-paid Zacks SCR** report (Zacks discloses up to $50K/yr in compensation from the issuer; its
  $3.50 price target should be read as promotional, not independent). That's a real flag and it's named here
  plainly. But it is **outweighed** by the opposite signal — a **Roth Capital-led underwritten public offering**
  (with Johnson Rice and D. Boral as co-managers) and an institutional base that includes Renaissance, Vanguard,
  Citadel, BlackRock and State Street. Regulated underwriters pricing a real deal and index/quant money holding
  the float is the *antithesis* of an OTC boiler-room. (Be honest about that institutional base, though: it's
  systematic/passive money, not conviction microcap funds — presence, not endorsement.) Net: real company with
  a promotional-research overlay. Flag both halves; it clears the gate.
- **Governance — disclosed, intertwined, worth watching.** Insiders own ~31%. There's a real related-party knot:
  **Synergy Offshore LLC**, owned by Chairman Weinzierl and director King, is owed **18% of cash realized from
  carbon sequestration** in the area of mutual interest (plus 18% of any gain on a plant sale). That's a genuine
  leakage on the 45Q prize (see §5) and a governance item — but it's disclosed and committee-managed, not hidden.

**The listing-risk flag — real and chronic, run it down.** USEG is a serial sub-$1 name: a **1-for-10 reverse
split (Jan 2020)** and **repeated Nasdaq minimum-bid deficiencies**, most recently a **Sept 2024 notice that it
cured in ~3 weeks without a split**. As of the latest filings (8-Ks through 2026-05-08) **there is no active
deficiency notice on file**, and the proxy carries **no reverse-split authorization**. But at ~$1.00 it sits
right on the line; a fresh minimum-bid notice — and the recurring threat of another reverse split — is a live
possibility to monitor on any post-May-2026 8-K. This is a structural nuisance, not a fraud tell.

**Verdict: PASS.** Real company, real assets, real contracts, credible operators, real capital. The promotion
overlay and the governance/related-party items are real and named — judgment items for you, not automatic no's.

## 3. Bull / base / bear — equity return shape (no LEAPS available)

**Bull (~3–5x+, a multi-bagger).** The plant commissions on schedule, first helium flows Q1-2027 into the
$285/MCF take-or-pay, the **EPA approves the MRV plans this summer** and the **~$130M of 45Q credits** start
accruing and get monetized via transfer, the residual Cut Bank oil holds, and **Phase 2 (a designed 2–3x
capacity step) gets sanctioned**. A ~$50M nano that proves out three contracted/credit-backed revenue streams
and re-rates toward a small-cap industrial-gas multiple is the moonshot — the issuer-paid Zacks DCF frames
~3.5x to $3.50; a clean execution + 45Q + Phase 2 path is where the 5x+ dream lives. This requires *everything
to go right*, which on a first-of-kind nano build is a lot to ask.

**Base (grind, modest re-rate, maybe ~1.5–2x).** The plant gets built but a quarter or two late; helium flows;
MRV approval comes but 45Q monetization ramps slowly and leaks 18% to Synergy plus a transfer discount; Phase 2
stays on the shelf for lack of capital. The stock works off the de-risking but the per-share gain is muted by
the share count and the slow cash conversion. Plausible middle path.

**Bear (this impairs, it doesn't just dip).** The ~$30M build is being funded by a ~$28M balance sheet with
almost no cushion (§5). A cost overrun beyond the EPC "defined contingency," a commissioning slip (already
nudged from Q3-2026 to "late 2026" in the company's own filings), or an MRV delay extends the burn and
**reopens the equity raise — at a sub-$1 price, into a base that already grew 27.9M → 52.3M shares in ~15
months.** Miss the **July 1, 2027 offtake outside date** and the investment-grade buyer gets a unilateral walk
right, vaporizing the single biggest de-risking fact. Worst case stacks several of these and the equity is
heavily diluted or impaired. The downside here is dilution and time, and there's no option to cap it.

## 4. What kills it — two real thesis-breakers.

**1) Dilution / the funding knife-edge (the dominant per-share risk).** The bull case assumes Phase 1 is funded
to first production. The math is *barely* true: ~$27.9M liquidity (cash + undrawn revolver) against **~$25M of
remaining Phase 1 capex front-loaded into the next 2–3 months** — leaving a ~$3M cushion **before** ~$2–2.5M/quarter
of operating burn through Q1-2027 (~$7M more). Add capex + opex and the need (~$32M) modestly *exceeds* the
$27.9M; the gap is bridgeable only by fully drawing the revolver (covenants are suspended through Q1-2027, so they
can) and deferring spend. **There is essentially no margin for a cost overrun or a slip.** Any stress comes out as
**equity** — the suspended Roth equity line still has ~15.3M shares / ~$15.9M of latent capacity, and shareholders
already removed the Nasdaq 20% issuance cap. The credit side is clean (no convertibles, no death spiral — that's
genuinely good), but the equity tap is the release valve, and it dilutes at a depressed price.

**2) The 45Q prize is contingent on one EPA stamp and on policy.** The carbon stream — **~2x+ the helium economics**
and the real reason to own this — is worth **$0 realized** until the **EPA approves both MRV plans (anticipated
summer 2026, but the timing has already drifted across four filings)**. And because USEG is a loss-making C-corp, it
can't use the credits itself — it must **sell them via §6418 transferability at a ~5–10% discount**, minus the 18%
Synergy carve-out. So the headline "$130M" is gross, undiscounted, pre-monetization, and gated. If the MRV approval
slips materially or 45Q policy is touched, the centerpiece of the valuation defers or shrinks.

## 5. The crux, made crisp

Three things, stated plainly, that decide this bet:

**(a) Today's ~$50M equity is essentially an option on completing a ~$30M build with a balance sheet that just
covers it.** On *in-hand, contracted* value — the helium take-or-pay (~$4.1M/yr gross) plus shrinking Cut Bank oil
(~$1.6M/quarter) — ~$50M is full-to-rich, not cheap. You are not buying hard cash flows at a discount; you are
buying **option value** on execution + 45Q + Phase 2. Price it as the option it is.

**(b) The helium contract is a VOLUME/EXECUTION re-rate, NOT a bet on the helium price.** The $285/MCF is **fixed**
(CPI-escalated only from March 2028, reset once in year 4). USEG sold the price upside to buy certainty. So a
helium-price spike does *not* flow to USEG — the upside is entirely about *building the plant and delivering
contracted volume*, then layering the bigger legs on top. And Phase 1 helium is **small on its own**: 1.2 MMCF/month
× 12 = **14,400 MCF/yr × $285 ≈ $4.1M/yr gross** (~8% of the market cap). The unit is unambiguous — MCF = 1,000 cf,
so "1.2 million cubic feet/month" and "1,200 MCF/month" are the same number; both filings corroborate ~$4.1M. Phase 1
helium is the **proof-of-execution and a contracted floor — not the prize.**

**(c) Carbon is the prize, and it's the more conditional one.** At Phase-1 scale, **45Q carbon ≈ $10.6M/yr gross
(125,000 t × $85)** vs **helium ~$4.1M/yr** — and over the contract lives, $130M of 12-year 45Q dwarfs ~$20.5M of
5-year helium. The carbon is ~2–2.6x the helium. But helium is *contracted and investment-grade-backed* (a hard
floor); carbon is *gated on the summer-2026 EPA approval, on the credit-transfer market holding at $85/t, on durable
IRA policy, and it leaks 18% to Synergy*. **Helium is the certainty; carbon is the upside.** The whole investable
thesis is: do they execute the build, clear the EPA stamp, and turn the contingent carbon resource into monetized
credits before the balance sheet forces another dilutive raise?

*(One framing correction for the record: the resource is a Ryder Scott **1C contingent** estimate — real and
named-engineer-backed, but the most speculative rung, not SEC-proved reserves. The only proved reserves are the
small legacy oil book (~1.45M BOE, ~$16.7M standardized measure). And the storage path is **Class II wells + Subpart
RR MRV**, not Class VI — relevant when reading the permitting catalyst.)*

### Bottom line

USEG is a **real, FID'd, contracted nano-cap** with a clean (non-toxic) balance sheet and a genuinely asymmetric
Bucket-1 shape — it clears the fraud gate decisively despite a yellow promotional-research overlay and a chronic
sub-$1 listing history. The bull case is real: an investment-grade take-or-pay helium contract as the floor, and a
**carbon/45Q prize worth ~2x the helium** that re-rates a ~$50M company hard *if* the summer-2026 EPA stamp lands and
the plant comes up on time. But two things define the bet honestly: **(1)** the ~$30M build is funded on a
knife-edge by a ~$28M balance sheet — any overrun or slip reopens equity dilution at a sub-$1 price into a share
count that already doubled in 15 months; and **(2)** the prize is contingent on one EPA approval that keeps drifting
right. This is an **equity-only** option on execution — small, real, and asymmetric, with the value living in the
carbon stream, not the (deliberately price-fixed, ~$4.1M/yr) helium. Watch the MRV approval and the build schedule
above everything else; respect that there's no LEAPS and no cushion if it slips.
