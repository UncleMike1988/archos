## 1. Is it real? — The one place to be strict here. Decisive PASS, but earn it; this name was *accused* of being fake.

Unlike Adobe (where the fraud gate is a formality), INOD is a name where the gate is the whole first
job, because the bear case for two years *was* "this is a hollow AI-washing promotion." Walk it
carefully — it clears, and the clearing is itself the catalyst.

**The accusations (real, serious, and now adjudicated):**
- **Wolfpack Research, 2024-02-15 — "Exposing Innodata's 'Smoke and Mirrors' AI."** Thesis: INOD is "a
  deteriorating, manual data-entry business driven by offshore labor, not innovation"; its "Goldengate"
  AI platform is "rudimentary software developed by a handful of employees"; ~96% offshore workforce;
  tiny R&D (~$4.4M over five years). Stock fell ~30.5% that day. This was an **AI-washing** thesis, not
  a revenue-fabrication thesis.
- **J Capital Research, 2024-09-23 and 2024-11-08.** Alleged the marquee "Mag-7" contract (which J Cap
  identified as **Meta**) "might be 90% overstated," that INOD "misled investors on $100M+ in
  commitments," and — in November — surfaced that INOD had received a **DOJ grand-jury subpoena
  (2024-08-07)** and SEC scrutiny, framing the subpoena as validation.

**The adjudication (this is the part that matters):**
- **Securities class action — DISMISSED.** *D'Agostino v. Innodata Inc.* (D.N.J., No. 2:24-cv-00971-JKS-JSA;
  lead plaintiff Francis Grondin; class period 2019-05-09 → 2024-02-14; §10(b)/§20(a)/Rule 10b-5
  "AI-washing" claims). On **2026-04-27**, Judge **Jamel K. Semper** (Doc 58, 36 pp) **granted** the
  motions to dismiss — for Innodata *and* for former part-time CFO Mark Spelker — on **failure to plead
  scienter** and the **inadequacy of the confidential-witness allegations** ("The Court cannot plausibly
  infer that the former employees had credible or firsthand knowledge…"). The court did **not** rule the
  AI statements were *true*; it ruled the complaint failed PSLRA pleading standards. **Important nuance:
  the dismissal was WITHOUT prejudice** — plaintiffs can theoretically re-plead — **but** this is the
  *third* straight failed pleading (original → FAC → SAC), the failure is on **scienter** (the hardest
  element to fix), and as of 2026-05-31 there is **no evidence of an amended complaint or appeal** (the
  Q1-26 10-Q describes it as a completed event). Residual tail risk: real but modest.
- **DOJ — CLOSED.** DOJ grand-jury subpoena received 2024-08-07; per the company's **8-K dated
  2025-06-12**, "the DOJ notified the Company that it has closed its investigation."
- **SEC — CLOSED, no enforcement.** Escalated from a March-2024 document-preservation letter to a formal
  September-2024 subpoena; same 2025-06-12 8-K: the SEC "concluded its investigation and does not intend
  to recommend an enforcement action."

**The substance test (the other half of "is it real"):**
- **Real revenue, real cash, real customers.** $251.7M FY2025 revenue (audited), $32.2M GAAP net income,
  $46.8M operating cash flow, $82.2M year-end cash rising to **$117.4M** by Q1-26 (helped by *customer
  prepayments on pretraining programs* — you don't prepay vapor). Five of the "Magnificent Seven" plus
  multiple frontier AI labs are customers; a **Palantir** engagement was named in Jan-2026.
- **Clean balance sheet — no toxic financing.** No convertibles, no death-spiral notes, no serial ATM;
  ~$9.3M of "long-term obligations" (largely legacy pension/severance), net cash ~$108M, an undrawn $50M
  Wells Fargo facility. Survives and self-funds without dilution.
- **Operators.** Founder-CEO **Jack Abuhoff** has run INOD for decades; this is not a narrative-hopper or
  serial promoter. (CFO is currently **interim — Marissa Espineli**; a permanent CFO search is a watch-item,
  not a red flag.)
- **Auditor — BDO, clean opinion.** Audited by **BDO India** (the India member firm of the BDO global

- **Organic vs manufactured.** No IBN/RedChip/wire-syndication footprint. Coverage is thin but *real*
  (Wedbush/Ives, Maxim, BWS, Craig-Hallum). The interest is carried by real frontier-lab spend and real

**Verdict: PASS, decisively.** This was the rare case where the market *tested* the fraud thesis with the

fact that the largest customer's identity is undisclosed (so the J-Cap "Meta-overstatement" claim can't be
*disproven* from outside, only weighed against the regulators walking away and the cash arriving). Nothing

## 4. What Innodata actually does — a human-expert + platform "data factory" for frontier model builders

INOD sits in the **training-data / model-improvement supply chain**, not in model building. The legacy
business was document/data engineering (the thing Wolfpack derided as "manual data entry"); the pivot —
now ~88% of revenue and essentially all the growth — is supplying the data and evaluation that frontier
labs and hyperscalers need to *build and harden* large models. Current stack (verified via IR + the Q1 call):

- **Pretraining data** — high-quality text at scale, including **graduate/PhD-level STEM datasets** (math,
  physics, chemistry, engineering, biology) to teach reasoning. Among the fastest-growing lines.
- **Mid/post-training data** — supervised fine-tuning, advanced-reasoning datasets, creative writing,
  **agent-improvement / agent trajectories**, alignment and **preference optimization (RLHF/human
  preference)**.
- **Model evaluation, red-teaming, trust & safety** — capability/alignment/safety evals; a new hyperscaler
  trust-&-safety engagement was cited at ~$3M annual run-rate.
- **Data engineering** — the legacy DDS backbone (transformation, curation, extraction, compliance).
- **Synthetic / off-the-shelf datasets** — resellable datasets where **INOD retains the IP** (a margin
  lever and a small step toward stickier, higher-margin product revenue).
- **Platforms (early):** a **Generative AI Test + Evaluation Platform** (LLM benchmarking + automated
  safety/red-team evals; built with **NVIDIA** NIM microservices, beta Mar-2025) and an **Agentic
  Evaluation & Observability Platform** (~$1M first deal in Q1, ~15 prospects evaluating). *(Note: the
  "Goldengate" name from the Wolfpack report could not be verified as a current product — the live eval
  product is the Generative AI Test + Evaluation Platform. Flag.)*

The proof this is "real AI work," not relabeled data entry: frontier labs are paying real, growing money
for it (the $51M land from zero; +453% on the rest; prepayments), and a major customer chose INOD for a
PhD-level-STEM reasoning-data program — work a "manual data entry" shop could not deliver.

## 5. Customer concentration — the crux, and the single thing that breaks the thesis

**The hard facts (Q1-2026, verified via release/10-Q/transcript + WebSearch):**
- **One customer = ~56% of Q1-2026 revenue.** A **second = ~17%.** **~65% of accounts receivable** sits
  with a single customer. (For trend: the top customer was ~58% of FY2025 and spiked to ~59% in Q3-2024.)
- The largest customer is a **Big-Tech / frontier-model builder** in the (former) DDS segment. INOD never
  names it — management uses "Big Tech," "hyperscaler," "one of the world's largest cloud and commerce
  companies," "frontier AI labs (~20 globally)."
- **Identity is genuinely unverifiable.** J Capital alleged it is **Meta** (and that the related commitment
  was overstated — a claim the DOJ/SEC declined to act on); a separate web guess floated **OpenAI**. Both
  are **unproven**. Treat the name as unknown; treat "Meta, 90% overstated" as a short-seller assertion the
  regulators walked away from.

**The diversification catalyst (both thesis claims VERIFIED on the call):**
1. **New Big-Tech customer → ~$51M in 2026, becoming customer #2.** Twelve months ago this customer was
   **zero**; this year it is guided to be the second-largest account, with "considerable headroom" in-program
   and other programs under discussion. **Confirmed.**
2. **Other Big-Tech customers +453% YoY in Q1** (aggregate, ex-top-customer). **Confirmed.**

**Is concentration improving?** Directionally **yes on a percentage basis, but the company is still acutely
single-customer-dependent today.** Management's exact framing: the largest customer will "represent a
decreasing percentage of total revenue even as our absolute dollar revenue with that customer expands… the
largest account continues to grow in absolute dollars, while the rest of the customer base grows even
faster." So the de-risking is real and measurable (the $51M #2 and the +453% cohort are the evidence), but
at **~56%** today the kill switch is live.

**What happens if the ~56% customer cuts 30%?** Roughly ~17 points of *total* company revenue evaporate in a
quarter — and because the revenue is project-based, not contracted backlog (§6), it can happen fast. At ~10x
forward sales there is no valuation cushion: revenue *and* multiple compress together. This is the dominant
risk in the file. The mitigant is that the diversification is visibly working *now* (not a promise), and a
frontier lab mid-program through pretraining→post-training→eval has real switching friction. But "the anchor
pauses or in-sources" is the thesis-breaker, and it is not hypothetical for a customer this size building its
own internal data teams.

## 6. Revenue quality — lumpy, project-based (not backlog), single segment now; margins expanding

**Recurring vs project-based — the soft spot.** This is **book-and-bill, statement-of-work revenue**, not
contracted recurring/MSA backlog. CEO Abuhoff, plainly: "There are things that we're doing that we won't be
doing next quarter. There are things we're gonna be doing next quarter that we're not doing this quarter."
INOD **does not give quarterly guidance** because work "starts and stops." Management argues the work is
becoming "increasingly of an ongoing nature… iterative, deeply embedded, structurally compounding" — but
**no backlog, committed-spend, or MSA dollar figure is disclosed**, and **deferred revenue is only ~$7.5M**
(at 12/31/25) against a ~$350M run-rate — i.e., negligible pre-contracted revenue. Visibility comes from
*pipeline and prepayments*, not contractual lock-in. This is the structural reason for the volatility, and it
compounds the concentration risk: a lumpy book + a 56% customer = quarters that can swing hard.

**Segments — now a single reporting segment.** Effective Q1-2026, INOD **collapsed its three historical
segments (DDS / Synodex / Agility) into one** (the CEO now reviews results consolidated). So going forward you
will **not** get the segment split — a transparency *reduction* worth flagging, though the rationale (agentic
tech now embedded across all three) is plausible. The last clean split (FY2025) shows how concentrated the
engine is:

**Margins — expanding, which is the quality tell.** GAAP gross margin (revenue − direct operating costs) was

The expansion is driven by IP-retained off-the-shelf datasets (resale economics), platform leverage (more
revenue per head), and synthetic-data generation. Adj-EBITDA margin hit **28% in Q1** — genuine operating
leverage on a services model. Model ~40–44% GAAP / ~44–47% adjusted GM, with upside if the product/dataset
mix keeps growing.

## 7. The competitive landscape — the Scale-AI/Meta dislocation is a real tailwind, and INOD is *a* beneficiary (not the winner)

The "Scale AI is now conflicted, so frontier labs diversify to neutral vendors" narrative is **real and
well-documented**, and it is the single biggest tailwind behind INOD's 2025–26 acceleration. (Honest caveat:
INOD management did **not** mention Scale or Meta on the Q1 call — the linkage is analyst/trade-press
inference, but the timing and the +453%/$51M data corroborate it.)

**The dislocation:** mid-2025, **Meta took ~49% of Scale AI (~$14.3B) and hired founder Alexandr Wang.**
Scale's largest customer (Google) planned to split; OpenAI began phasing Scale out; Microsoft and xAI
reportedly pulled back — all over conflict-of-interest fears (roadmap exposure to Meta). That shook loose
exactly the neutral frontier-lab spend INOD targets.

| Competitor | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale AI** | Impaired by the Meta conflict | Still large, bleeding neutral-lab work |
| **Surge AI** | The real winner | ~$1.2B+ rev, bootstrapped/profitable; reportedly raising at up to ~$25B |
| **Mercor** | Fast riser | ~$10B valuation (Oct-25); expert-network model (doctors/lawyers/scientists) |
| **Turing** | Growing | RL/expert data, same lane |
| **Appen (ASX:APX)** | Structural decline | Lost the ~A$83M Google contract (2024); old crowd-labeling model disrupted |
| **Labelbox / iMerit / Toloka / Invisible / Sama** | Mid-tier | platform + crowd/expert variants |

**Where INOD fits:** the **only liquid public pure-play** on the theme, a neutral mid-scale specialist
(~$90M/qtr) competing with the private expert-data leaders (Surge, Mercor, Turing) for frontier-lab
post-training/eval work, while legacy DDS competes with the old outsourcers (Appen, iMerit). It is **far
smaller than Surge and Mercor** and is *a* beneficiary of the Scale fallout, **not** the category winner —
but the scarcity value (nothing else listed offers this growth profile) is exactly why the market pays up.

**The moat / in-sourcing risk.** INOD's edge — a scaled, vetted PhD-level expert workforce, secure delivery,
speed/elasticity, multi-phase embedding, and nascent platforms/IP-retained datasets — is **a credible
service advantage, not a hard moat** (no network effect, no data lock-in). Frontier labs *do* build internal
data teams, and well-funded neutral specialists compete for the same work. The platforms (eval/observability)
are the attempt to build something stickier but are early (~$1M deals). The in-sourcing/disintermediation risk
is the structural counterpart to the concentration risk.

## What kills it — the one or two things that break the thesis

the kill switch. **Watch:** the top-customer % each quarter; any language change on that account; AI-capex
digestion across the labs.

forward sales / ~33x forward EV/EBITDA, ~9% off an all-time high reached days ago, the stock is priced for
40–50% growth to persist. A deceleration to "merely" 25–30% — entirely plausible as the base scales and the
lumpy book bites — would compress the multiple toward 5–6x sales and round-trip a chunk of the move, even
absent a customer loss. The insider behavior (CEO + COO selling ~$75M+ discretionarily into the spike, zero
buys) is the market-structure echo of this risk.
