## 1. Is it real? — YES. A real, NVIDIA-backed, $46B-contracted AI-cloud with real institutions; the Russia/sanctions gate clears cleanly. This is the most-scrutinized section here, and it passes.

Because the brief explicitly asks whether the Yandex→Nebius transition was clean, who owns the
company now, and whether any Russia/sanctions exposure remains, this gate is *not* a formality —
but it resolves clean on every limb:

- **The Russian assets are fully divested.** Per the 20-F, on **Feb 5, 2024** the company signed a
  definitive agreement to sell **all of its Russian businesses and related international
  operations** ("Divested Businesses") — which were **>95% of 2023 consolidated revenue and ~95% of
  assets and employees** — to a purchaser consortium. It closed in two steps: first close **May 17,
  2024** (68% stake; consideration ~RUB 237.5B paid in CNH ≈ **$2.4B** *plus* 67.6M of the

  final close **July 12, 2024**. Total ≈ **$5.2B**, the largest corporate exit from Russia. The
  remaining international AI/cloud entity was renamed **Nebius Group N.V.** and resumed Nasdaq
  trading in **October 2024**. There are **no Russian operations remaining**; data centers are in
  Finland, the U.S., Iceland, France, the UK, Israel, etc.
- **The founder's personal sanctions were lifted *before* he returned.** Arkady Volozh (Yandex
  founder, now Nebius CEO) was added to the EU sanctions list in **June 2022** and resigned his

## 2. Which bucket? — Honestly, NONE cleanly. It is a graduated, mega-cap *catalyst-grower* (Bucket-2 DNA at ~12x the Bucket-2 ceiling) — emphatically **not** a Bucket-3 de-rated compounder.

The brief floats "Bucket 2-3 hybrid, could push Bucket 3 given the size." The charter says to

- **Not Bucket 1 (sub-$1B moonshot):** at $58.7B it is ~60x over the ceiling. (It *was* a Bucket-1
  shape at the ~$3.3B Oct-2024 relisting — and it already ran the +1,300% moonshot leg; that's why

- **Not Bucket 2 ($1-5B catalyst grower) by size,** but it is **Bucket-2 in spirit**: a real company
  past the fraud/nano risk, growing on a genuine catalyst (the hyperscaler-contract land-grab), with
  a credible 2-3x-on-a-catalyst *return shape*. It has simply graduated to mega-cap scale while
  keeping the catalyst-grower profile.
- **Not Bucket 3 (de-rated compounder), and this is the key correction to the brief.** Bucket 3 is
  the ADBE/$NOW shape — a *profitable* quality name *sold off* on a fear while fundamentals compound,
  where the thesis is "multiple *normalization*, not expansion," and where LEAPS work because IV is
  low. NBIS is the **photographic negative** of that: it is **near all-time highs (not de-rated)**, it

  its **IV is very high (LEAPS punishing, not cheap)**. Calling it Bucket 3 because it's large would
  invert the entire point of the bucket.

**So: it fits none of the three cleanly.** The truthful one-liner is a *scaled-up Bucket-2
catalyst-grower / hyper-growth AI-infrastructure capex story, trading at a momentum multiple near
all-time highs.* That classification matters because it tells you the right lens: this is a
**second-leg, execution-and-funding bet at a rich price**, not a "buy de-rated quality and wait for
normalization" bet. The instrument is **equity** (§13).

## 3. Revenue quality — the $399M is real, recurring cloud revenue. The "net income" is not real profit. Both halves of that sentence matter.

This is the brief's load-bearing question, and the Q1 disclosures are unusually clean on the good
half and unusually important on the bad half:

**The good half — revenue is real recurring AI-cloud, not hardware resale or one-time items.**
Segment detail (Q1-FY2026 vs Q1-FY2025, $M):

| Segment | Q1-25 | Q1-26 | YoY | Share of Q1-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nebius AI cloud** | 41.4 | **389.7** | +841% | **97.7%** |
| TripleTen (edtech) | 10.5 | 11.6 | +10% | 2.9% |
| Avride (autonomous) | 0.2 | 0.9 | +350% | 0.2% |
| Eliminations | (1.2) | (3.2) | — | — |
| **Total** | **50.9** | **399.0** | **+684%** | 100% |

The Nebius AI cloud line is GPU compute, storage and networking recognized **as services are
provided** under **on-demand "pay-as-you-go" and fixed "reserved capacity" contracts** — i.e., real
cloud-services revenue, not reselling hardware and not intercompany (intersegment use is eliminated).
Growth was "driven by capacity scaling and supported by strong pricing and utilization." Toloka (AI
training-data) was **deconsolidated in Q2-2025** and is now an equity-method stake, so it is *out* of
revenue entirely; ClickHouse is likewise an equity stake, not consolidated. So reported revenue is
now essentially a **clean AI-cloud number** with two small attached businesses (edtech + a nascent
autonomous-driving unit). **ARR = $1.92B** (defined as *Nebius AI cloud* revenue in the last month of
the quarter × 12; $1.92B ÷ 12 ≈ $160M March cloud revenue), up from $1.25B at YE-2025 (**+54% QoQ,
+674% YoY**). The watchlist's "$1.92B ARR / +54% QoQ" was exactly right.

**The bad half — the GAAP "net income" is a non-cash mark-up, and even adjusted, they lose money.**
The Q1 statement of operations:

| Line ($M) | Q1-25 | Q1-26 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 50.9 | 399.0 |
| Total operating costs (incl. **D&A 212.0**) | 171.2 | 527.0 |
| **Loss from operations** | **(120.3)** | **(128.0)** |
| Interest expense | — | (63.7) |
| **Gain on revaluation of equity securities (ClickHouse)** | — | **+780.6** |
| Net income from continuing ops | (104.3) | **621.2** |

The eye-catching **+$621.2M "net income" is almost entirely the +$780.6M non-cash revaluation of the
ClickHouse stake** after a third-party round repriced it; strip it out and the business ran a
**−$128.0M operating loss** and paid **$63.7M of interest**. The company's own cleaner metric,
**"adjusted net loss," was −$100.3M in Q1** (vs −$83.6M a year earlier — it *widened*). The same
mechanic explains the **FY2025 "+$82.5M net income"**: it's ClickHouse mark-ups, not operations.
**Group adjusted EBITDA was +$129.5M (32% of revenue)** and the **Nebius AI-cloud segment hit a 45%
adjusted-EBITDA margin** (up from 24% in Q4) — a real operating-leverage story — *but* "adjusted
EBITDA" excludes the **$212M/qtr of D&A (53% of revenue) that is the true cost of the GPU model** and
all SBC. The honest read: **the core cloud is scaling toward genuine cash generation, but the
consolidated company is not profitable on any un-engineered basis, and the "net income" headline
should be ignored.**

## 4. Customer concentration — high, and *intensifying* into 2026, not diversifying. This is a top-two risk.

The brief asks whether Meta/MSFT/NVDA are customers or investors. Precisely: **Microsoft and Meta
are the two anchor *customers*; NVIDIA is an *investor* (and supplier).** Concentration is real:

- **FY2025 revenue concentration (20-F):** Customer A = **25%**, Customer B = **15%** → **top two ≈
  40%** of revenue (Customer C was 11% in 2024, fell below 10% in 2025).
- **Receivables concentration is even starker:** at 12/31/2025, **one customer (D) was 83% of gross
  accounts receivable (~$597M)** — almost certainly Microsoft, whose contract signed Sept 2025 and was
  ramping (large receivable, only a partial-year revenue contribution, so it didn't yet clear the 10%
  *revenue* bar but dominates the *balance*).
- **2026 makes it worse, not better.** The Microsoft (~$17.4B) and Meta (up to $27B) deals are the
  growth engine, so the top-two share of revenue almost certainly *rises* through 2026. The deferred-
  revenue book — **$4.78B** (current $685.6M + non-current $4,092.5M, up from $1.58B at YE) — is
  overwhelmingly these two prepaying hyperscalers.

**How to weigh it.** Two-sided. The bull reading: these are *contracted, take-or-pay, prepaid*
commitments from the most creditworthy buyers on earth — a ~$46B backlog that de-risks the revenue
ramp and *funds* the capex (§5). The bear reading is the one the charter wants named plainly: **a
neocloud's customers are also the parties building the in-house silicon (Azure Maia, Meta MTIA) meant
to displace rented GPUs**, and if a single hyperscaler's own buildout catches up and it doesn't renew
at term, a huge slice of contracted revenue is at renewal/refinancing risk. CoreWeave shows the
template and the danger: Microsoft was **67% of CRWV's 2025 revenue**. NBIS is less concentrated than
that *today* (~40% top-two) but is headed toward heavier hyperscaler dependence, not away from it.

## 5. Capex reality check — the central tension. $20–25B of 2026 capex on $3.0–3.4B of revenue, funded by a stack that still has a multi-billion hole.

This is where the brief's instinct is correct and the number is **real** (verified on the call, not a
misread): management **raised FY2026 capex guidance to $20–25B**, up from the **$16–20B** set in
February. Context to make the number legible:

- **It is 2026 *calendar-year* capex, not a multi-year cumulative.** FY2025 capex was ~$5B; Q1-2026
  alone was **$2.47B**. So the guide implies a **4–5x step-up** in annual spend.
- **It is a deliberate build-*ahead*.** CEO Volozh framed the raise as "investments in our 2027
  capacity that will come online early next year" — dollars spent in 2026 to light up capacity that
  generates revenue in **H1-2027**. That's why $20–25B looks absurd against $399M/qtr: it is **~7x
  capex/revenue**, a land-grab spend rate, not a return ratio.

**Where the money comes from — a five-part stack, and it does *not* fully close:**
1. **Cash on hand: $9.30B** at 3/31/26 (just raised).
2. **Customer prepayments:** $3.198B received in Q1; management is "very focused on generating
   prepayments... to reduce the capital needed from equity and debt." This is the cleanest source —
   the hyperscalers are pre-funding the build.
3. **Convertible debt:** ~$10B principal already outstanding (§6); plus a *forthcoming* raise —
   management said it plans to "start tapping... asset-backed financing and corporate-level debt" by
   "raising mid-single-digits billions of dollars in the near term."
4. **ATM equity program:** 25M Class A shares authorized (Nov 2025), **unused** to date — a dilution
   lever held in reserve.
5. **Operating cash flow** (which is currently positive only *because* of the prepayments).

**The honest gap.** The CFO said **"more than 90% of the CapEx range we projected in February
[$16–20B] is already secured by cash and contractual commitments."** Read precisely, that means ~90%
of the *old, lower* range is covered — the **incremental step up to $20–25B is *not yet funded*** and
depends on the not-yet-closed "mid-single-digits billions" debt raise (and/or more prepayments, and/or
the ATM). **The rumored "$7.5B debt raise" in the brief could not be verified against any primary
source** — only a *forthcoming, unquantified* asset-backed/corporate raise is referenced; treat
"$7.5B" as unconfirmed. So the capital plan is credible and largely sourced for the base build, but
the top of the guide carries **real financing risk** in a market where neocloud debt is getting more
scrutiny. **This is the single biggest swing factor in the thesis** — more than competition, more
than concentration.

## 6. Balance sheet & dilution — net-cash today, but a ~$10B convertible stack (in-the-money) and the NVIDIA warrants are a real future-share overhang.

Balance sheet at 3/31/26 ($M): cash **9,298** vs total debt **~8,450** (current 18.4 + non-current
8,432) → **net cash ~$848M**; P&E (GPU fleet, net) **7,132**; non-marketable equity securities
(ClickHouse + Toloka) **1,614**; **deferred revenue 4,778**; total equity **7,242**. Liquidity is
strong *right now*. The dilution picture is the watch-item:

- **Convertible notes — ~$10.04B principal / $8.43B carrying, six series**, all settling in **Class A
  shares**, conversion prices clustered ~**$180–183**:
  - June 2025: 2029 notes (2.00%), 2031 notes (3.00%)
  - Sept 2025: 2030 notes (1.00%), 2032 notes (2.75%)
  - **March 2026: $2.5875B 1.25% due 2031 (conv. ~$183.22) + $1.75B 2.625% due 2033 (conv. ~$180.31) =
    $4.3375B** (announced $3.75B → upsized → greenshoe → closed Mar 20).
  - The low coupons (1.0–3.0%) are *cheap cash* — the price Nebius pays is **equity optionality**. With
    the stock at ~$231, **all of these are in-the-money** (conv. ~$180–183), so on a fully-diluted
    basis ~$10B / ~$181 ≈ **~55M potential new Class A shares (~+25% of Class A)** if/as they convert.
- **NVIDIA prefunded warrants:** **21,065,936 Class A shares for $2.0B** ($0.0001 exercise, Mar 11,
  2026) — economically already shares (~8% of Class A), and a deep validation signal (NVIDIA's cost
  ≈ $95/share vs $231 now). *Correction to our own lore:* the oft-cited "$700M NVDA PIPE" is
  imprecise — the **Dec-2024 round was $700M *total*** (NVIDIA's slice ~$33M); **the $2.0B March-2026
  warrants are NVIDIA's material stake.**
- **Fully-diluted share count** (≈254M economic + ~21M warrants + ~55M convert shares ≈ **~330M**)
  pushes the fully-diluted cap toward **~$76B** — so the "~$58.7B" headline understates the claim on
  the equity. Dilution is the structural cost of funding a $20–25B build with cheap converts.

Net: a **fortress of liquidity today, financed by a dilution machine** — sustainable while the equity
is high and converts stay cheap, fragile if the stock falls below conversion and the debt market
re-prices neocloud risk.

## 7. Competitive position & moat — a real, *narrow* edge (NVIDIA alignment + vertical integration + a nascent software layer) on top of a structurally tough, commoditizing business.

Where NBIS sits, with the closest public comp:

| | **NBIS** | **CoreWeave (CRWV)** |
|---|---|---|
| Q1-26 revenue | $399M | **$2.08B** (~5x NBIS) |
| Backlog/RPO | ~$46B committed | ~$99.4B (company-reported) |
| Market cap | ~$58.7B | ~$59.8B |
| EV/trailing sales | **~36x** | ~7x |
| Top-customer concentration | top-2 ~40% | Microsoft **67%** |
| Profitability | op-loss; adj-net-loss | larger GAAP net loss (−$740M Q1) |

So **NBIS is the smaller, far-higher-multiple, more-vertically-integrated name** — the market is
paying ~5x CRWV's sales multiple for NBIS's faster growth rate (+684% vs +112%), NVIDIA alignment,
European/sovereign positioning, and a software-layer story. The private field (Lambda ~$760M rev,
Crusoe building the 1.2GW Abilene Stargate, **Nscale raised at $14.6B with a ~$14B Microsoft deal**,
Fluidstack/Google-backstopped, Vast Data at $30B) shows the capital flooding in — and that **NVIDIA
is an investor in nearly *all* of them** (the "circular financing" thread).

**What genuinely separates NBIS from "we have GPUs" (the moat, honestly weighed):**
- **NVIDIA relationship — the strongest, most verifiable pillar.** $2.0B NVIDIA equity (Mar 2026),
  **Reference-Platform NVIDIA Cloud Partner** status, **NVIDIA "Exemplar Cloud" status on GB300
  NVL72**, and a path to **>5GW of NVIDIA systems by 2030.** Equity alignment + priority allocation +
  reference architecture is hard to replicate — *and is also the dependency* (see bear case).
- **Vertical integration** — Nebius designs its own servers/racks/data-center layouts (claims ~20%
  less power than third-party hardware). Credible TCO edge vs pure GPU-renters; the specific
  performance/cost claims are company-sourced marketing.
- **Software/inference layer** — **Nebius Token Factory** (production inference-as-a-service, ex-AI
  Studio) plus the **Tavily** acquisition (AI search APIs) — the move up-stack to higher-margin,
  stickier revenue. Real product, **early** — 97.7% of revenue is still raw cloud.
- **European / sovereign-AI footprint** — Netherlands-domiciled, Europe-rooted; a genuine data-
  residency/sovereignty edge vs US-centric CRWV (though Nscale/Fluidstack chase the same demand).

**The bear case on the *business model* (this is where the real risk lives — not fraud, fundamentals):**
- **"Renting depreciating chips with borrowed money."** Neoclouds buy GPUs with debt, rent them, and
  must keep raising new tranches for each new generation; the sector holds **$20B+ of GPU-backed
  debt**, all leaning on a **5–6-year depreciation assumption**. Amazon already **cut server life
  6→5yr (Feb 2025)** citing AI's pace — and (see §10) **NBIS went the *other* way (4→5yr)**, which
  *flatters* its near-term margins exactly when peers are getting more conservative.
- **GPU-rental commoditization.** H100 rates fell from **$7–10/hr (early 2024) to $2–4/hr (late
  2025)** — a 50–70% collapse in revenue per GPU-hour. Raw compute is a price-taker business; without
  the software layer actually scaling, margins compress as supply catches up.
- **Obsolescence + NVIDIA dependence.** Blackwell laps Hopper economics by year 3 (squeezing the back
  half of the depreciation schedule where the debt assumes residual value); and the NVIDIA alignment
  that is the moat is also a single point of allocation dependence.
- **Hyperscalers are both the demand *and* the threat.** They rent today because they physically
  can't build fast enough (the shortage runs into mid-2026); the in-house silicon (Trainium/TPU/
  Maia/MTIA) is the lagged structural pressure on the rent-a-GPU bridge.

**Verdict:** the moat is **real but narrow** — it rests on staying NVIDIA's favored partner and on the
software layer converting into meaningful sticky revenue, neither guaranteed. The business is good and
growing fast; it is not a wide-moat compounder, and the multiple prices it like one.

## 8. The Aschenbrenner signal — meaningful but widely misread. It is a *hedged thematic infra-long*, not a concentrated conviction bet — inside a fund that is *short the chip complex NBIS depends on*.

The brief asks whether this is high-conviction or a basket bet, and "context matters" — it matters a
lot. Verified from the primary 13G and the Q1 13F:

- **The position:** Situational Awareness LP (principals **Leopold Aschenbrenner** and **Carl
  Shulman**) filed a Schedule 13G on **2026-05-27** (event date May 19) reporting **12,410,060 Class A
  shares = 5.6%** — a **new, post-Q1 position** (~$2.6B at disclosure).
- **The context that changes the read:** the fund is **~$13.68B** (Q1-2026 13F, 42 positions), and its
  *disclosed* book is a **barbell** — its **five largest holdings are all PUTS on the AI-hardware
  complex** (SMH, NVDA, ORCL, AVGO, AMD ≈ **$8.45B, ~62% of the book**), while its **largest disclosed
  long is Bloom Energy (~$879M)**, with longs skewed to **AI power/energy/infrastructure**. The thesis
  is coherent: *the value in AI accrues to the power and infrastructure layer, not the chipmakers.*
- **What it actually signals:** NBIS (AI compute + an aggressive *power* buildout) is the new big
  long on the **infrastructure** side of that barbell — and, strikingly, **Nebius just signed a $2.6B
  deal with Bloom Energy, the fund's #1 long (§9)**, so the positions are thematically linked. At
  ~$2.6B it's a large, real, ~19%-of-book bet by a credible AI-native investor — **but it is one leg
  of a hedge, not an all-in conviction wager**, and it sits inside a fund **explicitly short NVDA/
  AVGO/AMD — the very chip complex on whose allocation NBIS depends.** So: a genuine positive
  data-point and a smart-money tell on the *infra-over-silicon* theme — **not** evidence that a
  concentrated genius is betting the farm on NBIS specifically. Weigh it as confirmation of the theme,
  not as a reason to skip the valuation work.

## 9. Power as the binding constraint — Nebius's clearest *operational* edge, with a visible execution gap to watch. The Bloom Energy deal is behind-the-meter speed-to-power.

In AI infrastructure, **megawatts are the scarce input**, and Nebius is competing on speed-to-power:

- **The Bloom Energy agreement (signed May 14, announced May 20, 2026):** **328 MW of installed
  solid-oxide fuel cells (~250 MW guaranteed), behind-the-meter, on-site, expected operational in
  2026**, under a service structure of **up to $2.6B across three ten-year phases (a conditional
  ceiling, not an unconditional commitment).** Bloom owns install/O&M; Nebius buys the output. The
  point is **bypassing multi-year grid-interconnection queues** — energize GPUs *now* via on-site
  natural-gas fuel cells rather than waiting on utility transmission. (Cleaner than combustion, not
  zero-carbon. Some secondary coverage ties the cells to the Independence, MO campus; the primary
  release says only "U.S. sites.")
- **Capacity, defined in three tiers (per the Q1 shareholder letter):** *contracted* power (secured
  land + commitments), *connected* power (built/equipped DCs), *active* power (consuming/revenue-
  generating).
  - **Contracted: now >3.5 GW** (beat the 3 GW year-end goal) → **guidance raised to >4 GW by YE-2026**
    (the ladder went >1GW Aug-25 → >2.5GW Nov-25 → >3GW Feb-26 → >4GW now); **>75% owned** (capital-
    heavy, not asset-light).
  - **Connected: only 800 MW – 1 GW targeted by YE-2026.**
  - **Active: ~170 MW at YE-2025.**
- **The execution gap to watch:** the chasm between **>3.5 GW contracted and ≤1 GW connected by
  year-end** *is* the risk — contracts and ground-breakings are not revenue until power is connected
  and GPUs are installed and utilized. Sites: Finland (Mäntsälä ~75MW; Lappeenranta up to 310MW, 2027),
  New Jersey (up to 300MW), **Missouri/Independence (up to ~1.2GW, broke ground May 12, 2026, phase-1
  Q2-2027)**, **Pennsylvania (up to ~1.2GW, newly announced)**, Alabama (gigawatt-class), France/
  Iceland/UK/Israel/Spain. **Owned facilities targeted at 3 GW across five sites.** Management itself
  says Q3-2026 is when "capacity added... significantly expands our footprint" — so the **back-half
  ramp is the proof point**, and it is the same back-half ramp the revenue guide depends on.

## 10. Earnings-quality flags — collect them in one place; they're individually minor but collectively a reason to discount the optics.

Naming these together, per the charter (don't gloss a real problem because the story is exciting):
1. **"Net income" is a non-cash ClickHouse mark-up** (+$780.6M Q1; the FY2025 +$82.5M too). Operations
   lose money; **adjusted net loss −$100.3M Q1**. Ignore the headline EPS.
2. **Server useful-life extended 4→5 years, effective Q1-2026, prospectively** — this *lowers*
   depreciation expense and flatters margins precisely as Amazon and others *shorten* lives citing AI
   obsolescence. Defensible (utilization commitments) but the direction is convenient and aggressive.
3. **Two unremediated material weaknesses** — over **fixed assets** and **revenue recognition** (§1):
   the two areas that matter most for this exact business.
4. **Mid-tier auditor (Reanda) for FY2024–25**, upgrading to **Deloitte for FY2026** — a positive
   trajectory, but the audited-by-a-non-Big-Four years are the ones with the material weaknesses.
5. **"Adjusted EBITDA" excludes the $212M/qtr of D&A** that is the real cost of the GPU model and
   **all SBC** — so the 32%/45% adjusted-EBITDA margins overstate true economics.
6. **Operating cash flow is positive only because of customer prepayments** ($3.2B in Q1) — a genuine
   strength (the hyperscalers fund the build) but not the same as self-funding profitability.

None is a fraud flag; together they say *discount the GAAP optics and underwrite the cash economics
and the funded capex plan instead.*

## 12. The "already ran" question — is there a second leg, and what drives it?

The charter's framing is the right one here: a +1,300% mooonshot is *behind* this, so the question is
the **second leg**. The bull second-leg driver is concrete and datable: **ARR scaling from $1.92B
(Q1) toward the $7–9B YE-2026 guide** as the Microsoft/Meta capacity connects and the next gigawatt
sites (Missouri/Pennsylvania) come online in 2027 — a ~4x ARR move in 12 months, plus the software/
inference (Token Factory) margin story layering on top. If they hit $7–9B exit ARR at a 30–40%
adjusted-EBITDA margin, today's ~$58B EV is **~7–8x exit-ARR** — not demanding for that growth, and
the equity re-rates as the "can they fund/build it" doubt resolves. **That's a real second leg.**

But "it ran" here is *not* the same as "it's just higher than it was" — on fundamentals it is also
**genuinely richly priced** (above mean PT, ~36x trailing sales, fully-diluted ~$76B, GAAP-loss-
making), and the second leg is **gated on three things that can each slip:** funding the top of the
$20–25B capex range, connecting >3.5GW of contracted power into ≤1GW of live capacity on schedule,
and keeping hyperscaler concentration from becoming a renewal cliff. **So: yes, a credible second leg
exists and it's catalyst-datable (the H2-2026/H1-2027 capacity ramp), but you are paying a full price
for it with execution and financing risk still live — the opposite of the "buy it cheap while it's
hated" setups in the rest of the book.**

## 13. Instrument — equity. Options exist and are liquid, but IV is punishing; LEAPS are not the efficient expression here.

Unlike the sub-$1B names in the book, NBIS has a **deep, liquid options market** (Susquehanna, Jane
Street, Citadel are top "holders" precisely because of options market-making) — so liquidity is *not*
the constraint. The constraint is **implied volatility**: this is a stock that moves 10%+ on a
headline and whose converts price in high vol, so **LEAPS premiums are expensive** (the watchlist's
"~100% IV, LEAPS unusable" is directionally right — re-pull the live chain to confirm). A long-dated
call would need a large further move just to cover the extrinsic premium, and the re-rate has **no
hard date** (it's gated on the multi-quarter capacity ramp). **Equity is the clean expression of this
thesis** — it captures the full second-leg ARR ramp with no time decay, no IV bleed, and no
forced-exit-date, which matters when the catalyst is a 2026–27 build-out rather than a single print.
*(Note the converts are a built-in source of leverage/dilution at ~$180–183 — the company has already
sold the upside-participation that a LEAPS buyer would be paying up for.)* Verify the live chain
before assuming any structure.

## Bull / base / bear — equity return shape

Anchors: 2026 guide $3.0–3.4B revenue / $7–9B exit ARR / ~40% adj-EBITDA margin; ~$46B contracted
backlog; ~$58B EV; ~$195 mean PT; ~$231 spot.

**Bull (~$330–430, ~45–85% upside; "the build works and the multiple holds").** Capacity connects on
schedule, 2026 lands at/above the top of guide, exit ARR hits $8–9B, the software/inference layer
starts showing margin, the incremental capex gets funded without a punishing equity raise, and the
market keeps paying a premium growth multiple (~mid-teens forward sales / high-single-digits exit-ARR)
for the fastest-growing AI-infra name with NVIDIA in the cap table. The Citi/Citizens $270–287 targets
live here; a clean execution year plus continued hyperscaler land-grab could push beyond.

**Base (~$200–260, roughly flat to +12%; "great company, full price").** They hit the guide
*adequately* — ARR into the $7B+ zone — but the connected-vs-contracted gap causes some timing slip,
the funding gets done with some dilution, and the multiple **compresses toward the ~$195 mean PT** as
the market stops paying ~36x trailing for a name that's now better understood. You make money roughly
in line with ARR growth net of dilution and multiple normalization — i.e., the stock *grows into* its
valuation rather than re-rating up. This is the most likely path and it is **not very asymmetric from
~$231** — you're buying a great business at a price that already credits a lot.

**Bear (~$90–150, ~35–60% downside; "financing and/or the model bite").** Any of: the top of the
$20–25B capex doesn't get funded cheaply and they do a large dilutive equity raise (or the convert/
ABS market re-prices neocloud risk); GPU-rental economics commoditize faster than the software layer
scales; a hyperscaler's in-house silicon starts displacing rented capacity and a contract renewal
comes into question; or the H2 capacity ramp slips and the $7–9B ARR guide is missed. The stock is
GAAP-loss-making and ~36x trailing sales near all-time highs, so a multiple de-rate to Morgan
Stanley/Cantor's $129–144 zone — or below if funding turns hostile — is entirely on the table. The
converts falling out-of-the-money (<$180) would compound the financing problem.

**Shape:** **asymmetric to the *downside* from here**, which is the uncomfortable truth the rest of
this analysis builds to. The business is excellent and the second leg is real, but at ~$231 — above
the mean PT, ~36x trailing sales, fully-diluted ~$76B — you are paying a full price for a flawless
execution-and-funding year, while the downside (financing gap, commoditization, concentration,
dilution) is large and live. That is the inverse of the ADBE/Bucket-3 setup, where the price was the
friend.

## What kills it — the one or two things that break the thesis

**1. The capex can't be funded cheaply.** A $20–25B build on $3.0–3.4B of revenue with only ~90% of
the *lower* $16–20B range secured means the company is structurally dependent on continuous access to
cheap capital — prepayments, low-coupon converts, asset-backed debt. If the neocloud debt market
re-prices (the "$20B+ of GPU-backed debt on a 5–6yr depreciation assumption" narrative cracks), or the
stock falls through the ~$180 conversion levels, the funding mix shifts to **dilutive equity at a
lower price** — the classic capital-intensive-growth death spiral. **This is the thesis-breaker, and
it's a *financing* risk, not an operations risk.**

**2. The hyperscaler-concentration / commoditization pincer.** Microsoft + Meta are ~$46B of backlog
*and* the two parties building the silicon meant to displace rented GPUs; pair a renewal/utilization
disappointment from one of them with the ongoing collapse in GPU-rental rates, and both the revenue
ramp *and* the unit economics deteriorate at once — exactly when the depreciation schedule (just
*extended* 4→5yr) is most exposed.

*(Secondary: a botched/expensive equity raise; a material-weakness-driven restatement; the connected-
vs-contracted execution gap missing the H2 ramp; or simply multiple compression toward the ~$195 mean
as the market re-rates a richly-valued, GAAP-loss-making name. None alone is fatal, but each caps the
upside the price already assumes.)*
