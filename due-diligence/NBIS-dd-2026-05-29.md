# NBIS — Nebius Group N.V.

**Due diligence — 2026-05-29**
NASDAQ: NBIS · Amsterdam-based AI cloud ("neocloud") operator · ex-Yandex carve-out · foreign filer (20-F / 6-K)
Price at today's close: **$231.09** · Market cap: **~$53–58B** · ~254M shares · ~100% IV (equity-only — see note)

A quick correction before anything else: the working brief carried NBIS at "~$20B+ cap." That's stale by roughly 2.5x. At ~254M shares and today's $231 close, the company is **~$55B** — it was ~$53.8B at Monday's $208 close before this week's pop. That number matters, because it changes which bucket this is and how much asymmetry is actually left. Everything below is built on the real cap.

---

## 1. Is it real? — Yes, emphatically. This is the easiest PASS Archos Equities has run.

This is not a "confirm there's a there there" exercise. NBIS is one of the most real companies in the entire AI-infrastructure complex. The check is almost backwards here: the question isn't whether it's real, it's whether anything is left in the price.

**Real revenue, real and accelerating.** FY2025 group revenue $529.8M with *positive* net income of $82.5M and $384.8M operating cash flow ([20-F, filed 2026-04-30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926052948/0001104659-26-052948-index.htm)). Q1 2026 (reported 2026-05-13): group revenue **$399M** (+684% YoY, +75% QoQ), of which the Nebius AI cloud business is $390M — 98% of the company. Core AI-cloud ARR hit **~$1.9B**, up ~50% from ~$1.25B the prior quarter. Adjusted EBITDA swung to **+$130M**, with the AI segment at a 45% adj-EBITDA margin. FY2026 guide reiterated: **$3.0–3.4B revenue, $7–9B ARR, ~40% group adj-EBITDA margin.** (One caveat for honesty: Q1 *reported* net income of ~$621M exceeds revenue and is flattered by non-operating fair-value gains on equity stakes — read adj-EBITDA and ARR as the real operating signal, not the headline net income.)

**Named, signed, marquee customers — the strongest validation that exists.** Not LOIs, not vapor. The bellwethers are naming *them*, which is the exact reverse-direction tell that separates a real company from a name-dropper:
- **Meta** — up to **$27B over 5 years** ($12B dedicated capacity + up to $15B additional), signed March 2026, capacity online from 2027 ([CNBC, 2026-03-16](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/16/meta-nebius-ai-infrastructure.html)).
- **Microsoft** — up to **$19.4B over 5 years** (~$17.4B firm), signed ~Sept 2025.
- **NVIDIA** — **$2B equity** infusion (prefunded warrants on the balance sheet).
- **Bloom Energy** — **~$2.6B** fuel-cell power partnership, three 10-year phases, first phase 328MW (250MW guaranteed), deploying from 2026 ([CNBC, 2026-05-21](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/21/bloom-energy-nebius-ai-infrastructure-firm-partnership.html)).

Combined contracted backlog is **just over $46B**. Owned data centers in Finland, Missouri, and a new ~1.2GW Pennsylvania campus; contracted power guided toward **>4GW**.

**Credible operators — builders, not promoters.** CEO **Arkady Volozh** founded Yandex and built its cloud; the team is the ex-Yandex engineering core. CTO Danila Shtan, CRO Marc Boroditsky. This is a 20-year operating company that shed its Russian assets and re-emerged as a clean, US-listed, well-capitalized entity — the textbook "fresh start" carve-out, with the original builders still running it.

**Promotion check — entirely organic, the opposite of a pump.** Coverage is real financial press (CNBC, the analyst desks, Seeking Alpha) and the validation is real capital (Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA contracts; a credible $13.7B fund taking 5.6%). There is zero paid-IR infrastructure here — no IBN/RedChip wire spam, no "compensation for placement." (For contrast in one line: the VIVO pitch borrowed NBIS's credibility through a fake adjacency. NBIS is the real thing VIVO was name-dropping.)

**One honest asterisk, not a fraud flag:** insiders have been net sellers — roughly 16 sells vs. 4 buys over the trailing 90 days, ~$217M net, a steady stream of Form 4 / Rule 144 filings. For a recent carve-out where early holders are hitting their first liquidity windows after a 5x, a lot of that is mechanical diversification. But it's worth seeing plainly: not everyone on the inside is leaning *in* at these prices.

## 2. Which bucket? — Honestly, none of them cleanly. And that's the most useful thing in this memo.

The brief guessed Bucket 2 ("real $20B+ catalyst grower… at the larger end"). That guess rested on the stale $20B cap. At the real **~$55B**, it doesn't fit:

- **Bucket 2 (catalyst growers, $1–5B):** NBIS has Bucket 2's *character* exactly — real company, past fraud/nano risk, growing fast on a catalyst, equity-only. But it's **~10x past the size ceiling.** The whole point of the $1–5B line is that asymmetry lives where the company is still small and ignored. NBIS is neither.
- **Bucket 3 (de-rated compounders, up to ~$200B):** NBIS fits the *size* ceiling but violates everything Bucket 3 is *for*. Bucket 3 is the quiet, de-rated, lower-IV name that sold off on a fear that didn't materialize — where a calm LEAPS works. NBIS is the opposite: up ~440% over twelve months, trading at/near highs, ~100% IV, no de-rating to buy.

So the honest call: **NBIS is a Bucket-2-in-character catalyst grower that has already grown into a Bucket-3-sized body, without any of Bucket 3's de-rated cushion.** Functionally it's the **purest, highest-beta AI-capex equity on the board** — bought after the move, not before it. The buckets exist to find asymmetry; at $55B and near highs, the asymmetry that was here at $10–15B is mostly spent. That doesn't make it a "no" — it makes it a different kind of bet than the buckets are tuned for, and the operator should price it as such.

## 3. Bull / Base / Bear — equity, rough return shape

**Bull (~2–3x over 18–24 months).** The buildout executes, ARR climbs toward the $7–9B guide, and the $46B+ backlog converts to recognized revenue at ~40% EBITDA margins. NBIS cements itself as a structurally essential neocloud and the de-facto European sovereign-AI compute layer. A $55B cap re-rating to $120–150B+ is a real path *if* the AI-capex supercycle keeps running. Note what's missing: this is no longer a 10x. The 10x already happened — you're underwriting the second leg, not the first.

**Base (roughly flat to ~1.5x, white-knuckle).** Execution stays strong but a lot is already priced after +440%. The stock consolidates and grinds with the sector, with violent ±15% swings on every earnings print and capex update (it moved ~+16% on the Q1 report and ~+8–11% on the Aschenbrenner disclosure). Fundamentals grow into a rich multiple rather than the multiple expanding further. You earn the volatility without much net progress for a while.

**Bear (this halves — it doesn't dip).** NBIS is the most leveraged single-name expression of the AI-capex trade in existence, and the leverage is literal. 2026 capex guidance was *raised* to **$20–25B** against **~$9.3B** of cash on hand; cumulative capex through 2028 runs somewhere in the **~$50–75B** range (sources differ; the magnitude is the point). The Q1 operating cash flow looks healthy only because it's stuffed with customer *prepayments* — this is not a self-funding machine. The buildout **requires two things to both stay true at once: the supercycle continues, and capital stays cheap and available.** If AI-capex sentiment cracks, or rates/credit spreads back up, or a hyperscaler pauses — NBIS can't quietly trim; it's mid-build with multi-gigawatt commitments and ~$8.4B of debt already on the sheet. It re-prices 40–60% fast. Layer in customer concentration (Meta + Microsoft are the bulk of the $46B backlog — one renegotiation is material) and you have a stock built to fall harder than the sector it tracks.

---

## 4. What kills it

1. **AI-capex sentiment cracking + capital access tightening — together.** This is the whole bear case in one line. NBIS is a leveraged wager that the supercycle continues *and* the capital markets stay open to fund a $50–75B buildout. Either one failing is enough. This is not a tail risk you can hedge away inside the position — it *is* the position.
2. **Secondary:** execution/timing slips on the gigawatt buildout (power, GPU supply, construction); customer concentration in Meta/Microsoft; and foreign-filer / governance quirks from the Yandex lineage (controlled company, the Volozh-era control structure, and the sanctions-shadowed history of the former parent — real, manageable, but not nothing).

## The Aschenbrenner stake — already public, already popped, and read the *whole* position

Leopold Aschenbrenner's **Situational Awareness** fund disclosed a **13G on 2026-05-27**: ~**12.41M Class A shares, a 5.6% stake** (~$2.6B at the time), making it NBIS's largest disclosed holder. The fund has scaled from $225M to ~$13.7B AUM in under two years. It was **not** in the Q1 13F (added after March 31), and the stock popped ~8–11% on the disclosure — visible in the tape: $208 close on 5/27 → $226 on 5/28. **Treat this as already-public and already-paid-for, not a fresh edge.**

The part that's actually the signal: Situational Awareness is reportedly **long the neocloud operators (NBIS) while holding puts against NVDA/AVGO/AMD/ORCL and a short against the semis** — i.e., long the picks-and-shovels operator while *hedging the broader AI-capex trade it depends on.* (That put/short characterization is reported, not something I could confirm in a filing — weigh it as reported.) If true, it's the tell: the smartest dedicated money in this trade is expressing it long-NBIS-but-hedged, not naked long. That's the exact posture the bear case above describes — own the operator, fear the macro.

## LEAPS — unusable here. This is an equity decision, full stop.

Do not let the options chain tempt you. With IV around **100%**, the time premium on any LEAPS is punishing, and — worse — this stock *gaps* on single headlines (±8–16% repeatedly in the last three weeks). The post-pop vol crush will torch a directionally-correct long-dated call: you can be right on direction and still lose on a LEAPS as IV deflates after the very catalyst you bought it for. The realized volatility alone makes long premium a bad structure. If NBIS belongs in the book at all, it belongs as **shares**.

## Portfolio context — not a recommendation, just making the concentration visible

The active book (NOW, CLSK, AISP, VLN) is already heavily AI-infrastructure-correlated. **NBIS is not diversification — it's more of the same macro bet, in its highest-beta, most capital-intensive form, bought after a 5x, while the most credible dedicated holder in the name is hedging the very trade it rides.** Every one of those positions tends to move on the same news (AI-capex sentiment, rates, hyperscaler spending plans). Adding NBIS doesn't spread the risk; it concentrates it and raises the beta of the whole book. That's a sizing question, and it's yours — Archos Equities is just putting the correlation where you can see it.
