## 1. Is it real? — Trivial pass. Run for form; it's over in a sentence.

Adobe is a ~40-year-old, $23.8B-revenue, Nasdaq-100 mega-cap audited by KPMG, with **$7.13B of
FY2025 GAAP net income**, **$10.03B of operating cash flow**, **89% gross margins**, ~30,000
employees, and de-facto-standard products (PDF/Acrobat, Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, the
Experience Cloud). The "is this a promotion?" gate that exists for nano-caps is irrelevant here:
no paid-IR footprint, no toxic financing, no narrative-hopping operators, real coverage from
every major sell-side desk, and broad (~85%+) institutional ownership across 2,282 13F filers
(~420M shares held; UBS AM top holder). Balance sheet is fortress-grade: $6.89B cash + STI,
$6.23B investment-grade debt, $7.28B deferred revenue (a *liability* that is really a forward
revenue book), retained earnings $47.2B. Insider activity over 90 days is "mixed-negative" but
immaterial — routine 10b5-1 sales and RSU settlement, net -$3.7M, nothing that reads as a signal.
Nothing trips the one hard rule. The entire job on Adobe is layer-two fundamental DD.

## 2. Which bucket? — Bucket 3 (de-rated compounder) with a real Bucket 2 AI overlay.

At ~$105B cap this sits squarely inside the Bucket-3 band (quality names up to ~$200B that sold

The reason it's Bucket 3 and not "just a big slow name": the de-rate is fear-driven, not
fundamentals-driven. Revenue, ARR, FCF, and margins all made new records *during* the drawdown.
That divergence — price down ~40% while the financials set records — is the entire Bucket-3 thesis
in one line.

## 3. Bull / base / bear — equity return shape (LEAPS overlay modeled in §12)

Anchor multiples: FY2026 guide is **$25.9–26.1B revenue, $23.30–23.50 non-GAAP EPS** (reaffirmed
at Q1). FY2027 Street is ~$26.5–27 non-GAAP EPS as buybacks + low-double-digit growth compound.
10-yr average forward P/E ≈ 28–30x; 2022 trough ≈ 18–20x; *today ≈ 11x non-GAAP.*

**Bull (~$420–470, ~60–80% equity upside; the "$NOW re-rate").** The disruption fear is revealed
as wrong — AI is *additive*: Firefly + GenStudio + the Acrobat/Express AI-assistant funnel convert
the 850M MAU base into paid AI seats and credit consumption, net adds re-accelerate, and the market
re-rates ADBE from a trough ~11x toward a still-discounted **17–19x** FY2026 non-GAAP. That's
**$400–445** on FY2026 numbers, **$420–470** giving partial credit to FY2027 EPS + a ~7–8%/yr
share-count reduction. A clean internal CEO appointment removes the governance discount. This is
not a heroic multiple — it's *half* the stock's own 10-year average and roughly the market multiple
— so the bull case is "stop pricing terminal decline," not "pay a growth premium."

**Base (~$330–360, ~27–38% upside; the de-rating simply unwinds halfway).** Growth stays ~10–12%,
AI monetization is real but gradual, the CEO question resolves without drama, and the multiple
normalizes from a panic ~11x to a merely-cheap **14–15x** non-GAAP. That's **~$330–350**, which —
notably — is roughly the **current ~$327 Street mean target** and implies the de-rate was an
overshoot that half-corrects. The buyback does meaningful work here: ~$10B/yr of repurchase at a
depressed price is ~8% net share-count reduction annually, adding ~8 points to EPS growth on its own.

**Bear (~$190–225, ~15–27% downside; the fear is *right*).** Generative AI genuinely compresses
demand for professional creative seats; Canva/Google/Runway take the long tail and the SMB/prosumer
tier; Firefly monetization disappoints relative to the credit-consumption hype; net adds decelerate
toward high-single-digits; and the multiple stays at — or below — today's trough because the market
concludes this is structural erosion, not a fear cycle. Pair that with an external-CEO hire that
signals a strategy reset, and you re-test the **$224 low or modestly below** (~10x non-GAAP on
flat-to-decelerating EPS). The downside is *cushioned* — by a ~10% FCF yield, a $26B recurring base,
and the buyback bid — but it is real, and it is the reason the stock is at 11x in the first place.

**Shape:** unusually symmetric for a mega-cap, but skewed favorably by the trough multiple — you're
buying a record-setting franchise near a decade-low valuation with a ~10% owner's yield and a
self-funding buyback. The variance is almost entirely *multiple*, not *fundamentals*; the
fundamentals have been the boring part.

## 4. AI-first ARR trajectory — is Firefly/GenStudio actually converting to revenue? Mostly yes, but small.

This is the load-bearing question for the bull case, and the Q1 disclosures are genuinely good:

- **AI-first ARR more than tripled YoY** (management's headline metric; per call commentary the
  AI-first book surpassed the ~$400M level — a figure sourced to the prepared remarks, not the
  press-release headline, so treat the exact dollar as management-disclosed rather than
  filing-audited). "AI-first" = standalone AI products (Firefly app, Acrobat AI Assistant, Express
  with AI, GenStudio) — i.e., revenue that *would not exist absent the AI build-out*.
- **Firefly ending ARR (app + credit packs + Firefly Enterprise) > $250M, +75% q/q**, with
  **generative-credit consumption +45% q/q.** The q/q acceleration is the tell that this is
  adoption, not a one-time pull-forward.
- **GenStudio ending ARR +30% YoY** (the enterprise content-supply-chain product — the real B2B
  monetization vector, where AI content generation plugs into Experience Cloud).
- **Adobe Experience Platform + native apps ending ARR +30% YoY**; **customers with >$10M ARR +20%
  YoY** — enterprise is spending *more*, not defecting.
- **MAU > 850M, +17% YoY** across Acrobat, Creative Cloud, Express, Firefly — the top of the funnel
  the AI seats and credits monetize against.

**Honest weighing.** The trajectory is real and accelerating, the IP-clean angle (see §10) is a
genuine enterprise wedge, and tripling/+75%-q/q growth is exactly what "additive, not destructive"
looks like in the data. *But* — keep the magnitude honest — **$400M of AI-first ARR is ~1.5% of a
$26B base.** The thesis is not "AI-first ARR is already moving the needle"; it's "AI-first ARR is
growing fast enough, off a real base, that within 2–3 years it becomes a visible second growth
engine *and* it proves AI expands rather than cannibalizes the franchise." Q1 is supporting
evidence for that, not proof of it. The June 11 print (Q2) is the next data point on whether the
q/q acceleration holds.

## 5. The "net-new Digital Media ARR miss" — the metric was *retired*; here's what actually happened.

The brief asks why net-new Digital Media ARR "missed in Q1 — timing or structural?" The precise,
verified answer reframes the question: **Adobe stopped reporting it.** Effective Q1 FY2026, Adobe
**collapsed its three historical segments (Digital Media, Digital Experience, Publishing &
Advertising) into a single operating and reportable segment**, and changed its internal Net-New
Sales Goal from "Digital Media ending ARR growth + Digital Experience subscription-revenue growth"
to **growth in *Total Adobe* ending ARR** (the two new customer groups — Creative & Marketing
Professionals, and Business Professionals & Consumers — combined). So the standalone "net-new
Digital Media ARR" line — the single most-scrutinized number on the stock through FY2024–25, and
the repeated trigger for post-print selloffs — **no longer exists as a reported figure.**

This cuts two ways and both are true:

- **Bull read (the company's framing):** the segment walls were artificial. Adobe now sells "one
  Adobe" — Creative + Document + Experience cross-sold through unified motions — so Total ARR
  ($26.06B, +10.9% YoY) and AI-first ARR are better forward indicators than a legacy sub-segment
  net-add. Subscription revenue +13%, RPO +13%, MAU +17% all corroborate a still-compounding engine.
- **Bear read (the transparency flag, and it's legitimate):** reducing disclosure on the *exact
  metric that was disappointing* — right as the stock was being punished for it — is a yellow flag.
  It makes the deceleration harder to see and harder to hold management to. Per the charter, name it
  plainly: this is the kind of reporting change that conveniently buries the bear's favorite number,
  and it deserves skepticism even though the unified-GTM rationale is also genuine.

**Timing or structural?** On the underlying question, the honest answer is *mostly structural
deceleration of a maturing $26B franchise (law of large numbers), partially offset by accelerating
AI-first lines* — not a cliff, but not re-acceleration yet either. Net new ARR gets mathematically
harder every year on a bigger base; the swing factor is whether AI-first ARR (§4) inflects total
net adds back up. That is precisely the thing the June 11 print and the next 2–3 quarters will
adjudicate, and it is the same thing the multiple is waiting on.

## 6. The $NOW parallel — structurally the same setup, but Adobe's bear case is more legitimate and the catalyst is murkier.

**What maps cleanly (the reason this is the playbook candidate):**
- A quality, sticky, recurring-revenue enterprise compounder **de-rated ~40–60% on a single
  narrative fear** while subscription revenue and cRPO kept compounding double digits. $NOW fell
  ~$830 → ~$340 (~-59%) in 2022 on "IT budgets get cut / rates kill software multiples"; ADBE fell
  ~$421 → ~$224 (~-47%) on "AI commoditizes creative tools."
- In both, **the fear was the entire story and the financials never confirmed it** — records were
  set during the drawdown.
- In both, **AI gets reframed from threat to tailwind** (ServiceNow's Now Assist upsell; Adobe's
  Firefly/GenStudio/AI-assistant monetization). $NOW re-rated to new all-time highs (>$1,000)
  through 2023–24 as Now Assist became a clean accretive upsell and the budget fear evaporated.
- In both, the **multiple compressed to a multi-year trough** and the re-rate is "the market stops
  pricing terminal risk," not "pay a premium."

**What's different — and these are the reasons to size the confidence lower than $NOW deserved:**
1. **The fear is more legitimate.** ServiceNow's fear was cyclical/macro (IT budgets, rates) and
   transparently transient. Adobe's fear is *secular/structural*: generative image and video models
   genuinely commoditize entry-level creation in a way IT-budget cuts never threatened a workflow
   moat. The bear case here is not stupid; it has to be *out-argued*, not just waited out.
2. **Slower base growth.** $NOW kept posting ~20%+ subscription growth through its de-rate; Adobe is
   ~11–13%. The re-rate therefore needs the *multiple* to do more of the work, and decelerating-
   growth optics (§5) cut against that.
3. **A CEO transition overhang $NOW didn't have** (§7) — open-ended, external candidates on the
   table.
4. **AI monetization is smaller relative to base and its pricing power is contested** (§9). Now
   Assist's accretion showed up fast and clean; Adobe's $400M AI-first ARR is ~1.5% of revenue and
   the Street is split on whether Adobe can charge a premium versus AI-first challengers.

**Net:** the *shape* is a near-perfect $NOW analog and that's why it's interesting; the *confidence*
should be lower than $NOW warranted, because Adobe's disruption fear is real rather than imagined,
the growth is slower, and there's a governance question gating the re-rate. The re-rate can absolutely
happen — but it's lower-probability and likely slower than $NOW's was, and partly hostage to the CEO
outcome.

## 7. CEO transition risk — real, open-ended, and a core piece of *why* the stock is cheap.

Verified from the Q1 earnings 8-K (2026-03-12): **Shantanu Narayen — 18 years as CEO, 28 at Adobe,
the architect of the cloud/subscription transformation that took Adobe from <$1B to >$25B revenue —
has decided to transition out of the CEO seat *after a successor is appointed*, while remaining
Chair.** The board formed a special committee chaired by Lead Independent Director **Frank Calderoni**
to run a formal search considering **both internal and external candidates**. **No target date** has
been stated.

Why this is a genuine overhang, not a footnote:
- **It's open-ended.** "After a successor is appointed" with external candidates in scope is the
  *least* reassuring form of transition — it is not a pre-baked internal handoff with a date. The
  uncertainty window could be 6–18+ months.
- **External candidates on the table** signals the board is at least entertaining a strategy reset.
  An external hire would likely be read by the market as "the board thinks the franchise needs
  re-pointing for the AI era" — a negative tape reaction even if it's the right long-run call.
- **The obvious internal frontrunners** are the two Presidents — **David Wadhwani** (President, the
  Creative/Document franchises) and **Anil Chakravarthy** (President, Digital Experience/enterprise)
  — both credible, long-tenured operators. A clean appointment of either would most likely *remove*
  a chunk of the discount. (Note: the board has not publicly named candidates; the two Presidents are
  the structurally obvious internal options, not confirmed finalists.)

**Read:** this is one of the two things actively suppressing the multiple (the other being the AI
fear). It is therefore also a *catalyst in disguise* — a clean internal succession is one of the
identifiable events that could trigger the re-rate. It cuts both ways and the timing is unknowable;
it belongs in the bull case (as a removable discount) and the bear case (as reset risk) simultaneously.

## 8. Semrush acquisition — small, strategically on-thesis, closes H1-2026, regulatory is the only watch-item.

Verified: announced **November 2025**, Adobe to acquire **Semrush (SEMR)** for **$12.00/share, all
cash, ~$1.9B equity value**, a **~77.5% premium** to the prior close. **Semrush shareholders
approved Feb 3, 2026.** Expected to **close in H1-2026**, pending regulatory clearance (US
Hart-Scott-Rodino plus UK/EU review given Adobe's Experience Cloud scale). Targets explicitly
**exclude** any Semrush contribution until close.

**What it adds:** Semrush is the leading SEO/online-visibility and digital-marketing analytics
platform — and, increasingly, a **"AI visibility / generative engine optimization" (GEO)** play:
measuring and managing how a brand shows up *inside AI answers* (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity), not
just in the ten blue links. That is directly on-thesis: it plugs brand-presence measurement into
**GenStudio + Experience Cloud**, extending Adobe's content-supply-chain story from *create →
activate* to *create → activate → measure-in-the-AI-surface*. It also brings a large SMB/marketer
base Adobe can cross-sell.

**How to weigh it:** strategically sensible and cheap relative to Adobe's balance sheet (~$1.9B vs
$6.9B cash + $10B FCF), not thesis-defining. The genuine risk is **regulatory** — Adobe's last
large deal (the $20B Figma acquisition) was *terminated in Dec 2023* on EU/UK opposition, costing a
$1B reverse-termination fee (which is what depressed FY2024 operating income and exaggerates FY2025's
+29% rebound, a nuance worth carrying). A $1.9B Semrush tuck-in is a far smaller antitrust target than
Figma, so the base case is it closes — but UK CMA / EU scrutiny of Adobe is now a known reflex, so
"closes on time" is not a certainty. Immaterial to the equity thesis either way.

## 9. Competition — the disruption thesis is *real but overstated*; the seat-model fear is the crux.

This is where the bear case lives, so weigh it without flinching:

- **Canva** — the most direct enterprise threat. Crossed **~$3.3–4B ARR growing ~35%**, last marked
  around a **~$42B** valuation (Aug-2025 employee sale; co-founder publicly defending an earlier $60B
  mark against competitive pressure), an IPO candidate. It owns the prosumer/SMB/marketing-team tier
  Adobe has always under-served, and is now AI-native (integrated Google Gemini, May 2026). Canva
  takes the *long tail*; the open question is whether it climbs into Adobe's professional core.
- **Google** — the most dangerous *model-layer* threat. Released **"Stitch," an AI-native "vibe
  design" tool** (March 2026: describe an objective/feeling, get generated design directions), and
  **Gemini image generation ("Nano Banana"-class models)** that commoditize raw image creation.
  Critically, though — **Gemini added Adobe, Canva, and CapCut as *native tools* (May 2026)**, i.e.,
  Adobe is being *integrated into* the AI ecosystem, not only disrupted by it.
- **Midjourney / Runway / OpenAI** — best-in-class generation quality (Midjourney V8 roadmap; OpenAI's
  image/video stack, with reports of Sora being de-prioritized — single-source, treat as soft).
  These pressure the *generation* layer but lack Adobe's editing depth, workflow integration, and —
  decisively — enterprise IP indemnity (§10). Midjourney offers **zero** IP indemnification.

**The honest synthesis.** The market's thesis — "AI compresses demand for professional creative
seats and crushes Adobe's pricing power" — is the legitimate bear case, and sell-side skeptics
(e.g., Melius) are explicit that customers may resist premium Adobe pricing when AI-first
alternatives do "good enough" at lower cost. That is the structural risk, and it is not refutable
on a spreadsheet. **The counter, which is also real:** (1) Adobe is repositioning Firefly as a
*model-agnostic aggregation layer* that runs third-party models (Google, OpenAI, etc.) *inside*
Adobe's safe, integrated, indemnified workflow — "be the trusted enterprise surface on top of all
models" rather than "win the model war"; (2) the deep editing/precision/collaboration moat in
Photoshop/Premiere/After Effects is not replicated by prompt-to-image tools; (3) enterprise +
government lock-in (§13) and IP indemnity (§10) are switching costs AI quality alone doesn't
dissolve; (4) the data so far (MAU +17%, >$10M-ARR customers +20%, AI-first ARR 3x) shows expansion,
not defection. **Verdict:** the disruption is real at the *bottom* of the market (commodity image
gen, prosumer/SMB) and overstated at the *top* (professional + enterprise + regulated). The stock is
priced as if the top is falling too. Whether that's wrong is the entire investment.

## 10. The Firefly IP-indemnity moat — durable and enterprise-relevant, but narrower than the marketing.

**How it works.** Firefly is trained on **Adobe Stock, openly licensed content, and public-domain
material** — deliberately *not* on scraped web images — which lets Adobe market it as
"commercially safe." Enterprise plans carry **full IP indemnification** (Adobe defends the customer
against copyright claims on Firefly *output*) plus dedicated legal support and SOC 2 compliance.

**Why enterprises actually care.** For an agency or Fortune-500 brand team shipping generative
content at scale, a **single IP dispute dwarfs the subscription cost** — indemnity is cheap legal
insurance, and a growing share of large enterprises now *require* licensed-training-data +
indemnification before approving any genAI tool. That is a genuine, enterprise-grade wedge, and it
is the clearest reason GenStudio/Firefly Enterprise win procurement against Midjourney/Stable
Diffusion. It is the part of the AI story least vulnerable to "the models got good enough."

**The durability caveats — name them honestly:**
- **The indemnity is narrower than the marketing copy.** It covers **copyright infringement of the
  output**; **trademark and right-of-publicity claims are generally outside scope**, and the **free
  Firefly tier carries no contractual indemnity** — only paid enterprise agreements do. Teams that
  assume blanket protection get caught.
- **It's no longer unique.** **OpenAI now indemnifies its enterprise/API customers** (not consumer
  tiers). The *category* of "indemnified enterprise genAI" is widening, so Adobe's edge is the
  *combination* — clean training data + indemnity + native integration into the tools creatives
  already use + the enterprise relationship — rather than indemnity as a standalone monopoly.

**Read:** a real, defensible, enterprise-specific moat that the disruption narrative ignores — but a
*wedge and a switching-cost*, not an impregnable wall. It durably protects the enterprise/regulated
core; it does little for the prosumer/SMB tier Canva and Google are taking.

## 13. Government / regulated installed base — a quiet, AI-proof moat the disruption narrative ignores.

Adobe is deeply embedded in U.S. federal and regulated-industry workflows, with a stack of **FedRAMP
authorizations** that take years and millions to obtain and effectively lock incumbents in:
- **Adobe Experience Manager (AEM)** + Adobe Connect — FedRAMP authorized at **Moderate** impact
  (HHS ATO since 2015, used government-wide); **AEM Edge Delivery Services** now supports FedRAMP
  deployments.
- **Acrobat Sign for Government** — **FedRAMP Moderate** authorized e-signature/document workflow.
- **Adobe Analytics, Adobe Campaign, Creative Cloud for Enterprise, Document Cloud (PDF Services +
  Sign)** — **FedRAMP Li-SaaS** authorized.

**Why it matters to the thesis:** PDF is a *de-facto government and legal standard* and Acrobat is
the default tool for it; FedRAMP + SOC 2 + the indemnity (§10) create switching costs that "the AI
image model got better" does nothing to erode. This is the most disruption-resistant slice of the
business — sticky, recurring, compliance-gated, multi-year — and it's a real reason the bear's
"seats evaporate" thesis is too broad. It doesn't show up in the headline AI narrative, which is
exactly why it's underpriced into a 11x multiple.

## 14. Buyback math — the self-funding floor that compounds the re-rate.

Verified: the board approved a **new $25B repurchase authorization on 2026-04-21** (through Apr 30,
2030), **on top of ~$3.89B remaining** on the prior program exiting Q1 → **~$29B of buyback capacity**.
Q1-FY2026 repurchases were **~8.1M shares for $2.478B** (~$306 avg, paid when the stock was higher).
Run-rate ≈ **$2.5B/quarter ≈ $10B/yr**, roughly Adobe's entire FCF.

The mechanics at a depressed price:
- ~$10B/yr ÷ ~$260 ≈ **~38M shares/yr gross** ≈ ~9.5% of ~402M shares; net of ~5–6M shares of annual
  SBC issuance, **~7–8% net share-count reduction per year.**
- Diluted share count has already gone **459M (FY23) → 450M (FY24) → 427M (FY25, wtd) → 411M (Q1-FY26)
  → ~402M guided (Q2-FY26)** — a ~12% reduction in three years, *accelerating as the price falls.*
- That ~7–8% annual shrink adds ~7–8 points to EPS growth on its own. Stack it on ~10–12% revenue
  growth and ~flat-to-up margins, and **non-GAAP EPS can compound mid-to-high-teens even with no
  multiple help** — the lower the stock, the *more* accretive each dollar of buyback, which puts a
  genuine floor under the de-rate.

This is the unglamorous engine of the Bucket-3 thesis: even if the market is slow to re-rate, the
buyback grinds EPS higher at a discounted price, so *time is on the holder's side* in a way it isn't
for a non-FCF-generative name.

## What kills it — the one or two things that break the thesis

**1. The AI fear is *right* — structural seat compression, not a fear cycle.** If generative AI
genuinely erodes demand for professional creative seats (not just the prosumer tail), net adds
decelerate toward high-single-digits, Firefly monetization stalls relative to the credit-consumption
hype, and pricing power slips as "good-enough" AI-first tools undercut Adobe. In that world the
multiple *stays* at 11x or lower because it's correctly pricing slow erosion, and the re-rate never
comes — you'd own a 10% FCF-yielding melting-ice-cube. This is the thesis-breaker, it's the reason
the stock is cheap, and it cannot be dismissed — only out-argued by the data (which, so far, shows
expansion). **Watch:** net-new Total ARR trajectory, AI-first ARR q/q acceleration, and any sign of
gross/net retention slipping in the Creative & Marketing group. June 11 is the next read.

**2. A messy CEO transition.** An external hire signaling a strategy reset, or a prolonged
no-successor vacuum, keeps the governance discount on (or widens it) and removes one of the catalysts
that would otherwise trigger the re-rate. A clean internal appointment is bullish; the open-ended,
external-inclusive search is the risk.

*(Secondary, lower-probability: Semrush regulatory drama given Adobe's post-Figma antitrust reflex —
immaterial to the equity; and macro multiple compression that hits all software — but at 11x ADBE has
already taken most of that medicine.)*

## The crux, made crisp: the rare case where the setup *and* the price agree — gated on two overhangs

Everything the charter wants in a Bucket-3 name is here: a dominant, fortress-balance-sheet, 89%-gross-
margin, ~$10B-FCF franchise that **set revenue, ARR, margin, and cash-flow records while the stock fell
~40–47%** on a single fear. It trades at **~11x forward non-GAAP / ~4x sales — the cheapest in a
decade and below the S&P 500** — against a 28–30x decade-average multiple, with a **~10% FCF yield** and
a **$29B self-funding buyback shrinking the count ~7–8%/yr** that compounds EPS even if the market never
re-rates. The AI story is, so far, *additive*: **AI-first ARR tripled, Firefly ARR >$250M (+75% q/q),
MAU +17%, >$10M-ARR customers +20%.** The IP-indemnity + FedRAMP + workflow moats protect the
enterprise/regulated core the disruption narrative writes off. This is the genuine "$NOW playbook":
de-rated quality, fear that the fundamentals contradict, AI reframed from threat to tailwind, multiple
at a trough waiting to normalize.

**What separates it from a layup is honesty about two things.** *First,* unlike $NOW's transparently
cyclical fear, **Adobe's disruption fear is structurally legitimate** — AI really is commoditizing the
*bottom* of the creative market (Canva, Google Stitch/Gemini, Runway), and the bear case is that it
climbs into the professional core; the stock is priced as if it will. *Second,* there are **two live
overhangs $NOW didn't have** — an **open-ended CEO search with external candidates on the table**, and
a **maturing core decelerating** (now partly obscured by the segment collapse that retired the
net-new-Digital-Media-ARR metric). The re-rate to $350 (15x) / $400 (17x) / $450 (19x) needs only
multiple *normalization*, not expansion — but it likely needs the AI-additive proof to keep printing
*and* the CEO question to resolve cleanly. **Net: the highest-quality, best-priced name in the book by
a distance, with the most legitimate bear case of the de-rated set. Lower-confidence than $NOW was, but
far more asymmetric than VPG — here the price is the friend, not the enemy.** The identifiable catalysts
are concrete and near (June 11 Q2 print; a clean internal CEO appointment; continued AI-first
acceleration), which is what a Bucket-3 entry is supposed to look like.
