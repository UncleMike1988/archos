### Nuclear Instrumentation Chokepoint Assessment

**Is the market concentrated?** Moderate concentration with a few large global players. Competitors include:
- **Leonardo DRS Nuclear I&C** (Class 1E safety-related, 70+ year heritage from Consolidated Controls)
- **Emerson** (50+ years nuclear-qualified instrumentation, all reactor technologies)

- **H3D** (imaging spectrometer niche)

**Sole-source positions?** Mirion's installed-base position from Paragon acquisition gives "presence in 100% of N.A. reactors." However, "presence" is not equivalent to "sole source." Multiple suppliers compete for new builds and retrofits.

**NRC regulatory moat:** Strong. Class 1E safety-related qualification typically takes **3–7 years** for a new supplier (full design qual + EQ testing + commercial-grade dedication). This is a real barrier but not insurmountable — multiple competitors have already cleared it.

**NDAA / regulatory mandate?** No NDAA-style domestic-sourcing mandate specific to nuclear instrumentation. ITAR controls apply to some defense products. The 2025 White House nuclear executive orders + DOE LPO support generally favor U.S. suppliers but are not specifically Mirion-protective.

**20+ SMR developer relationships — binding or non-binding?** Likely a mix:
- **Likely binding:** I&C content on commercial-launching reactors (NuScale, Kairos Hermes) where Paragon's commercial-grade-dedication services are already specified
- **Likely non-binding:** MOUs, framework agreements with earlier-stage developers (Last Energy, Oklo Aurora pre-commercial, X-energy demo, Holtec SMR-300, etc.) that will need to convert to firm POs as construction permits issue
- **Unknown without 10-K detail:** the breakdown is not publicly decomposed by counterparty in Mirion's disclosures

**Chokepoint verdict: MIR is an OLIGOPOLISTIC SUPPLIER with HIGH REGULATORY MOAT and STRONG INSTALLED-BASE POSITION — but NOT a strict chokepoint pure-play.** The 20+ developer coverage is more "supplier diversification across reactor designs" than "no one else can supply this." The Paragon acquisition meaningfully strengthens the moat but does not create monopoly economics.

### 2. Is the SMR backlog acceleration ($85M YTD vs $39M FY2025) a durable step-function or lumpy timing?

Morgan Stanley's "backlog stagnant over last 2 years" critique is a real bear-case flag.

MIR's competitive advantage is breadth (across all reactor designs + medical + defense) and installed-base lock-in (long aftermarket tails), not sole-source pricing power.
