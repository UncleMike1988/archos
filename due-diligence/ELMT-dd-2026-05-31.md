## 4. The China policy moat — the core bull thesis, and it's statutory, dated, and real (get the citation right).

This is the strongest, most verifiable leg of the case. The binding authority is **10 U.S.C. §4872**
("Acquisition of sensitive materials from non-allied foreign nations: prohibition"), built up by **FY2021
NDAA §844 + FY2024 NDAA §854 + FY2026 NDAA**, implemented through **DFARS 252.225-7052 / 225.7018** (DFARS
Case 2021-D015). *(The brief's "Section 833" is a related but secondary FY2023 specialty-metals disclosure
provision under 10 U.S.C. §4863 — not the binding covered-nations ban. Cite §4872.)*

- **Covered materials:** SmCo + NdFeB magnets, tantalum, **tungsten metal powder, tungsten heavy alloy**
  (and components containing them), and — added by the **FY2026 NDAA, signed Dec 18, 2025** — **molybdenum**
  (plus gallium, germanium). Both of Elmet's core metals are now covered.
- **Covered nations:** China (PRC), Russia, Iran, North Korea.
- **The date that matters — January 1, 2027:** today the prohibition applies to covered material *melted or
  produced* in a covered nation; **as of Jan 1, 2027 it expands backward up the chain** to any covered
  material **mined, refined, separated, melted, or produced** in a covered nation. After that date, even
  China-*mined* tungsten processed elsewhere is non-compliant for DoD — which structurally forces primes
  off Chinese-origin tungsten and toward U.S.-owned supply. **This is ~7 months out.**
- **Recycled exception:** allowed only if the recycled feedstock originated *outside* a covered nation *and*
  melting + all further processing happen in the U.S. or an ally.

Why this is a real moat and not marketing: the U.S. has **not mined tungsten commercially since 2015**, and
China is **~79–82% of global tungsten mine production** (USGS MCS 2026: 67kt of 85kt; the share eased from
~83% only because Kazakhstan's Boguty mine started up). A federal customer that legally cannot buy
China-touched tungsten, in a market China dominates, with a domestic vertically-integrating supplier
sitting right in the gap — that is the structural demand pull. The episodic overlays are softer and
two-directional: **China's own Feb-2025 dual-use export controls** on tungsten/moly (which drove Rotterdam
APT $331→$675/mtu) were **suspended for one year on Nov 10, 2025** as a trade-truce concession (so that
tailwind is *currently relaxed*); and the **Section 232 investigation on processed critical minerals**
found a national-security threat (Oct 2025) but the **Jan-15-2026 proclamation imposed no tariff yet** —
180-day negotiations leave a possible tungsten tariff by ~mid-2026 as upside optionality, not a present
fact.

## 5. The "only U.S.-owned vertically integrated" claim — true as carefully worded; "vertically integrated" on tungsten is partly a 2027 promise.

The S-1 wording, verbatim: *"We are the only U.S.-owned vertically integrated manufacturer of engineered
**pure** tungsten and **pure** molybdenum materials and products… Our CMC division maintains a supply chain
for raw materials outside of [China]… and controls every step of the manufacturing process… We sell these
metals at every stage of the process, from powder to final machined components."*

Four qualifiers are load-bearing — **(a) U.S.-owned, (b) vertically integrated, (c) *pure* tungsten AND
*pure* moly, (d) materials *and* products** — and the claim survives because every competitor fails at
least one leg:

| Competitor | Tungsten | Moly | VI powder→part | U.S.-owned | Why the claim survives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Global Tungsten & Powders** (Towanda PA) — largest Western W processor | ✔ | ✘ | ✔ | **✘ — Plansee (Austria)** | foreign-owned; W only |
| **Plansee USA / Mi-Tech** | ✔ | ✔ (group) | ✔ | **✘ — Plansee (Austria)** | foreign-owned |
| **H.C. Starck Tungsten Powders** | ✔ | ✘ | midstream | **✘ — Masan (Vietnam); pending sale to Mitsubishi (Japan)** | foreign-owned; also an Elmet *supplier* |
| **Kennametal** (NYSE: KMT) | ✔ (carbide) | limited | tooling, not pure-metal mill | ✔ US | carbide tools, not pure W+Mo engineered; not VI in moly |
| **Buffalo Tungsten** (Depew NY) | ✔ (powder) | ✘ | powder only | **✘ — Sandvik (Sweden), Dec 2023** | foreign-owned now; W only |
| **Molymet** (Chile) | ✘ | ✔ (world's largest roaster) | moly only | **✘ — Chile** | moly only; an Elmet *supplier* |
| **ATI** | exited (sold to KMT 2013) | — | — | ✔ US | left tungsten |

So the literal sentence is defensible — **no other U.S.-owned firm spans both pure tungsten and pure moly,
powder-to-part.** The foundation is the **Nov-2023 acquisition of H.C. Starck Solutions Americas**, which
the parties' own release said created *"the largest U.S.-owned producer of tungsten and molybdenum materials
and products"* — i.e., the moat was *bought* by bringing the former foreign-affiliated Starck Americas
operation under U.S. ownership.

**The spin to flag — "vertically integrated" on the tungsten side is partly aspirational.** Per the S-1,
Elmet does **not** yet convert its own tungsten concentrate to powder: concentrate → blue tungsten oxide
(BTO) is done at supplier **Masan's plant in Vietnam**, and a chunk of BTO → powder is **toll-converted at
a competitor's (H.C. Starck) plant in Sarnia, Canada**. Full in-house tungsten integration depends on a
**new furnace at Coldwater MI expected online ~Q1-2027** (notably, the same quarter the DoD ban bites).
Molybdenum integration is more genuine today (Elmet buys ADM and reduces it in its own ME/MI furnaces).
Read it as **marketing-true, structurally thinner than the headline, and completing in 2027** — the
build-out is itself the catalyst, not a sunk fact.

## 7. The financials and the Q1 inflection — the GAAP loss is an accounting artifact; the real story is margin + backlog.

**Q1-FY2026 (ended Apr 3) vs Q1-FY2025:** revenue **$56.0M (+20.7%)**; gross profit **$11.8M, 21.2% margin
(+260bp)**; operating income **$1.9M** (down YoY only because opex carried IPO/stock-comp costs — G&A jumped
$3.3M→$7.1M); **adjusted EBITDA $9.2M (16.4% margin) vs $4.5M (9.6%) — +106%**; **adjusted net income $4.7M
($0.24) vs $1.9M ($0.10)**.

**The GAAP net loss of $(0.3M) / $(0.02) is not an operating problem.** It is driven by a **$4.7M tax
provision that includes a $3.79M one-time charge from the S-corp→C-corp conversion** in the January
reorganization — a non-recurring, non-operating item. (A $3.1M non-cash gain on a derivative asset partly
offsets within other income.) Strip the conversion tax and the IPO costs and the quarter is solidly
profitable — which is exactly what the adjusted $0.24 EPS shows.

**Segments — the growth and margin story is CMC, not the microwave-weapons narrative:**
- **CMC (critical materials): $48.2M (86% of revenue), +23.2% YoY**, gross margin expanded **15.1%→20.3%**,
  operating income **$5.2M**. This is the engine and the margin lever.
- **EMP (microwave): $7.8M (14%), ~flat**, gross margin *compressed* **37%→26%**, swung to a **$(0.2M)
  operating loss**. The lumpier, currently-underperforming division.
- **Corporate/Other:** $(3.1M) operating drag (IPO/SBC).

**Quality-of-revenue signals are good:** over-time (long-term defense contract) revenue **tripled
$7.1M→$18.6M**, and **deferred revenue rose $10.1M (Dec-24) → $14.9M (Dec-25) → $23.5M (Apr-26)** —
customers are pre-paying to lock capacity/supply, the strongest forward-demand tell on the balance sheet.
**Backlog $113.3M (+52% YoY, +18% QoQ)** corroborates. **Customer concentration is moderate** — top
customer ~11% in Q1 (no single >10% for FY2025).

The honest counterweight: refractory metals are a **metal-content pass-through** business, so the **~6%
operating margin is structural** (gross margin in the low-20s, lots of expensive tungsten/moly flowing
through at low value-add). The Q1 16.4% *adjusted-EBITDA* margin is healthy for the category but partly
reflects favorable over-time-revenue timing/mix; **the Q2 print (~Aug 2026) is the first test of whether
the step-up holds or reverts toward the FY2025 ~11.6%.**

capital $62.3M (57%)**, working capital/GCP $26.9M (25%), **debt repayment only $19.8M (18%)**. The debt
repaid was the **insider/related-party** stack (Great Falls $15M, CEO LOC, AAI note) — see §1. So the bulk
of the cash is earmarked for capacity expansion (the Coldwater furnace etc.), which is thesis-consistent
(it funds the vertical-integration build) but means the raise was *not* a clean balance-sheet rescue.

**Balance sheet, pre→post:** pre-IPO total debt **~$50.3M** ($33.1M third-party + $17.4M related-party) as
of Apr 3. Post-IPO, after repaying the Great Falls term loan (Apr 28), CEO LOC (May 1), Symphony notes
(May 5), and part of the AAI note: **~$33M debt remains** (almost entirely the Wells Fargo
revolver/term against $75M inventory) against **~$100M cash → ~$67M net cash**, ~$162M equity. **Net cash,
self-funding, no toxic structure.** Inventory is the working-capital reality of refractory metals (tungsten/
moly tie up cash); the Wells Fargo revolver is the normal financing for it and likely stays.

## 11. Raw-material supply — non-China supply is contracted but thin; Taniobis is a niobium product line, not tungsten feedstock.

- **Tungsten:** concentrate from **Masan Tungsten (Vietnam)** + **EQ Resources' Saloro/Barruecopardo mine
  (Spain)** under a renewable 5-year offtake (2024); concentrate→BTO at Masan's Vietnam plant; BTO→powder
  split between Lewiston and **H.C. Starck's Sarnia (Canada)** toll plant (until the 2027 Coldwater furnace).
- **Molybdenum:** ADM from **Freeport's Climax mine (Colorado)** + **Molymet (Chile)** under long-term
  contracts.
- **Taniobis (JX Advanced Metals / JX Nippon, Japan):** a **niobium-alloy product collaboration** — Elmet
  sells/3D-prints **C-103** powders (and is developing a cheaper FS85) for aerospace/hypersonics. It is **not**
  a tungsten/moly feedstock relationship; the brief's framing was incorrect.
- **Concentration risk (real, S-1-acknowledged):** "there are only a small number of … non-PRC source
  suppliers … our business depends on a limited number of suppliers." Two vendors were ~25% and ~20% of AP
  in Q1. The non-China chain is secured *by contract* but with thin redundancy, and **Masan is simultaneously
  a key supplier and (via H.C. Starck) a competitor** — a structural awkwardness worth tracking.
- **Recycling:** notably *not* Elmet's stated feedstock strategy (unlike GTP/Starck), even though the §4872
  recycled-material exception would make domestically-melted non-China scrap a compliant DoD feedstock — a
  current gap / future opportunity.

**Honest correction to the China thesis — the moly leg is weak.** USGS MCS 2026: **China is only ~37% of
world molybdenum, and the U.S. is a *net exporter*** (Climax/Colorado + byproduct copper). So the moly side
of "critical materials" is a **defense-traceability/policy** story (the FY2026 NDAA coverage), **not an
ore-scarcity** story. The genuine scarcity moat is **tungsten** (zero U.S. mines since 2015, China ~80%).
And "100% import-reliant on tungsten" is precise only for *mined ore*; USGS pegs overall tungsten net import
reliance at ">50%" (recycling fills part).

## What kills it — the one or two things that break the thesis

**1. The October-2026 lockup floods supply and caps the stock.** ~20.6M insider/affiliate shares (~2/3 of
the company, including the founder, AAI, and Schott) become free against a ~9.86M float. If any large holder
monetizes — and the founder's intentions are untested since he sold nothing at IPO — the overhang can
suppress the price through the exact window the structural catalyst (Jan-2027 ban) is supposed to work. This
is the dominant mechanical risk and the most likely source of a bear-case re-test of $13.

**2. The Q1 margin step-up doesn't hold — it's a low-margin pass-through after all.** Adjusted-EBITDA margin
jumped 9.6%→16.4% partly on over-time-revenue timing/mix; if it reverts toward FY2025's ~11.6% while EMP keeps
dragging and growth-capex runs ahead of returns, the "+106% EBITDA" headline deflates, the multiple
compresses toward Kennametal's ~1.4x EV/sales, and the equity round-trips below the IPO. Refractory metals
are structurally ~6% operating margin — the bull case needs CMC scale + the furnace to lift that, and that's
unproven over more than one quarter.

*(Secondary: the moly leg of the China thesis is weak (US is a net moly exporter), so half the "critical
materials" story is policy-traceability not scarcity; the China export-control truce (suspended Nov 2025)
removed the APT-price tailwind; "vertical integration" on tungsten is a 2027 promise dependent on the
Coldwater furnace landing on time; and supplier concentration (Masan is both supplier and competitor) is a
real if-something-breaks risk.)*

## The crux

Everything the moonshot bucket wants is here, and the fraud gate is a non-event: a real, ~96-year-old,
profitable U.S. refractory-metals manufacturer — the *only* U.S.-owned firm spanning pure tungsten and pure
molybdenum powder-to-part — IPO'd clean (100% primary, no insider cash-out, single-class, reputable
underwriters, mainstream IR) at a non-demanding **~1.9x EV/sales** with **net cash**, riding a **federal
demand mandate**: as of **Jan 1, 2027** the DoD legally cannot buy China-origin tungsten (and the FY2026 NDAA
just added molybdenum), in a market China controls ~80% of and the U.S. hasn't mined in a decade. The Q1
inflection is the thesis confirming — **revenue +21%, adjusted EBITDA +106%, backlog +52% to a record $113M**,
all driven by the ADG book (Javelin/Hellfire/Sidewinder/hypersonics). The pattern is a textbook
`WINNER_UNIVERSE` shape: small, ignored (fresh IPO), real, just before a dated catalyst, with *the government
as the big-customer tell.*

**What separates it from a layup is honesty about four things.** *First,* this is a **structurally
low-margin** metal-content-pass-through business (~6% operating margin — normal for the category, proven by
Materion, but it caps the quality), and the eye-catching Q1 margin jump is only one quarter old and partly
timing. *Second,* the **moly leg of the China story is weak** — the U.S. is a net moly exporter, so half the
"critical materials" pitch is policy-traceability, not scarcity; the durable scarcity moat is tungsten alone.
*Third,* **"vertically integrated" on tungsten is partly a 2027 promise** — today it leans on a Vietnamese
supplier and a competitor's Canadian toll plant until the Coldwater furnace comes online (conveniently, the
same quarter the ban bites — so execution-on-schedule matters). *Fourth, and most actionable,* the
**~Oct-19-2026 lockup frees ~2/3 of the company into a ~9.86M-share float** — the dominant near-term
overhang, in the exact window the structural catalyst is supposed to work. The MP-style 31x re-rate needs a
government *capital* event (DoD stake / price floor / Section-232 tariff) that **does not exist yet**; the base
case is a "specialty-metals-with-defense-growth" re-rate from ~1.9x toward 3–5x EV/sales, which is the $20–21
sell-side zone and a real 1.5–3x. **Quality is solid-not-elite (margin-capped); the catalyst is genuine,
dated, and government-mandated; the entry is reasonable but the price is still discovering itself 5 weeks in,
and the cleanest risk-adjusted entry is *after* the October lockup clears — into the Jan-2027 mandate — not
before it.**
