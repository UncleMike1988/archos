# SDGR — Schrödinger, Inc. (Nasdaq)

*DD date 2026-05-29 · price/figures verified at run time (2026-05-30) against the Q1-2026 10-Q,
the Q1-2026 press release/8-K, the May 5 2026 earnings call, and options contract reference data.*

**Snapshot.** ~$15.2/share, ~$1.1B market cap (~74.5M shares). Cash + marketable securities
$406M, **zero financial debt** → EV ~$725M. Down ~45% from a $27.63 52-week high. A dual-engine
business: physics-based (FEP) drug-discovery software + enterprise informatics (LiveDesign,
Maestro), bolted to a milestone/royalty drug pipeline. This is the de-rated-quality setup the hunt
flagged, and the numbers hold up to scrutiny — with two honest caveats below.

---

## 1. Is it real? — Clean pass.

Not close to a question here. Real product, real revenue ($58.6M in Q1 alone), real named
blue-chip customers (the top-20 pharma roster runs FEP/LiveDesign), real cash, real scientists.

- **Customers & validation.** On Jan 9 2026 Eli Lilly chose Schrödinger's **LiveDesign as the
  "priority interface"** to make its own internal AI models (**TuneLab**) available to outside
  biotechs via federated learning. The direction of attribution is the right one — a top-3 pharma
  routing its AI *through* SDGR's software, not SDGR claiming adjacency. (See the crux section for
  what this does and doesn't mean for revenue.)
- **Balance sheet.** $406M cash, no debt, ~$15M *operating* cash burn in Q1 (the $60M net loss is
  mostly non-cash — stock comp, D&A, and a fair-value mark on equity investments; the hosted
  transition also pulls cash forward via deferred revenue). At ~$15M/quarter operating burn the
  runway is multi-year before you even count Ajax cash. Management guides to EBITDA profitability
  by 2028. Survives easily without toxic financing.
- **Operators.** CEO Ramy Farid (computational chemist, long-tenured) and founder/chairman David
  E. Shaw (D.E. Shaw, a genuine pioneer of molecular simulation). Scientists, not promoters.
- **Promotion check — organic, full stop.** Holders include the **Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation
  Trust**, David Shaw (~17%), BlackRock, Vanguard (~6%), MFS, Lazard; ~94% institutional, 19
  covering sell-side analysts with a Buy consensus. No IBN/RedChip/MZ footprint, no paid placement,
  no cashtag spam. The bull case is carried by real capital and real coverage.

Nothing here trips the one hard rule. Move on.

## 2. Which bucket? — Bucket 3, with a Bucket-2 pipeline overlay.

A de-rated quality compounder: a real, well-capitalized franchise that sold off ~45% on a fear
(shrinking reported software revenue) while the underlying franchise and its marquee relationship
got *stronger*. The pipeline — ~$700M cash already banked from equity/BD, up to ~$5B of potential
future milestones and royalties across 15 programs — is the Bucket-2 catalyst layer stapled on top.
The one place it diverges from the textbook Bucket-3 name: **there are no LEAPS** (see instrument
note), so the usual "2.5–3.5x on an 18-month LEAPS" expression isn't available. This is an equity
decision.

## 3. Bull / base / bear — equity return shape

**Bull (~2.5–3.5x, back toward the high-$30s–$50s).** The hosted-rev-rec transition laps in
2026–27 and reported software revenue inflects from down ~21% to growing, *visibly* reuniting with
the ACV line. TuneLab-in-LiveDesign and the new **Bunsen** agentic-AI product (early access summer
2026, throughput-based pricing) broaden the user base and push ACV growth from low-teens back toward
high-teens. Pipeline milestones/royalties start landing as partnered programs advance. The multiple
re-rates off a washed-out ~3.6x-ACV level. This requires *reacceleration*, which is not yet in the
numbers.

**Base (~1.5–2x, back toward the ~$21–27 analyst range).** The rev-rec optics simply clear: the
market stops penalizing a revenue line it eventually understands is a deliberate accounting shift,
software keeps compounding ACV at low-teens, the Ajax cash lands, and the stock drifts back toward
the analyst average ($21 / median ~$25 / up to ~$27). No heroics — just the fear not materializing.
This is the most probable path.

**Bear.** ACV growth *also* rolls over — not just reported revenue — confirming the de-rate was
demand, not accounting. Then it's a GAAP-unprofitable software-plus-biotech-lottery with no
near-term earnings, no reacceleration, and **no LEAPS to cap the downside** — uncapped equity risk
back toward the $11 low. Secondary bear drivers: partnered-pipeline clinical failures zeroing
milestone optionality, a slow or renegotiated Ajax close, and biotech/SaaS multiple compression
dragging the whole cohort down regardless of execution.

## 4. What kills it? — One real risk, one structural one.

**The thesis-breaker: ACV growth stalling.** The entire de-rated-quality call rests on the claim
that the −21% reported software revenue is rev-rec optics, not demand erosion. ACV — annualized
value of active contracts — is the truer signal, and it's still *growing*: +12% in Q1 ($28.4M vs
$25.4M), trailing ACV ~$201M, FY26 guide $218–228M (10–15%) maintained. **But it is growing at
low-teens, decelerating from SDGR's historical high-teens/20%+.** So the floor (ACV still up) holds;
the upside (reacceleration) is *not yet visible*. If the next two prints show ACV flattening, the
compounder thesis is gone — watch the trailing-ACV line above everything else.

**The structural one: no LEAPS, GAAP losses.** You can't cap downside with a long-dated option here
(longest listed expiry is Dec 2026), and you can't value the company on earnings. It's equity, held
through the burn, betting the franchise re-rates before patience runs out.

## The crux, made crisp: ACV vs. reported software revenue

This is the single most important thing to get right, so here it is plainly.

**The fear (what de-rated the stock):** software revenue fell **21% YoY to $35.6M** in Q1.

**Why that's substantially an accounting artifact, not lost demand:**
- SDGR is deliberately shifting customers to **hosted/subscription** licenses, which recognize
  revenue *ratably* over the term, versus legacy on-prem term licenses that booked a big slug
  *upfront*. Hosted is now **34% of software revenue, up from 24%** a year ago. Mechanically, a
  faster hosted mix *compresses reported revenue even as bookings rise.*
- **Software gross margin fell from 80% to 69%** — exactly what a real hosting transition does (you
  take on cloud-infra cost). That's a transition signature, not a demand signature.
- **Operating cash burn (~$15M) ran far below the $60M net loss** — consistent with healthy upfront
  hosted collections flowing into deferred revenue. A company losing customers doesn't collect cash
  ahead of revenue.
- The bookings signal, **ACV, kept growing (+12%, guide reaffirmed at +10–15%).**

**The honest counter:** "+12% and a 10–15% guide" is *low-teens* — slower than SDGR's prior
high-teens. The rev-rec read is right (the de-rate is overdone), but the data shows *steady*
software, not *reaccelerating* software. The base case (drift back to ~$21–27) only needs the fear
to clear; the bull case (2.5–3.5x) needs ACV to inflect upward, and that hasn't happened yet.

**On valuation (per the charter — name it honestly):** EV ~$725M against ~$201M trailing ACV is
**~3.6x recurring software value**, or ~3x total revenue. For a 69–80%-gross-margin franchise still
growing double-digits, with the entire ~$5B pipeline-milestone optionality and the Ajax cash
essentially thrown in free, that is **cheap, not expensive** — the opposite of a 40x-sales problem.
The valuation supports the thesis rather than fighting it. What you *are* paying up for is patience:
no GAAP profit until ~2028.

## The near-term catalyst: Ajax cash

Eli Lilly is acquiring **Ajax Therapeutics** for **up to $2.3B** to get AJ1-11095, a Type-II JAK2
inhibitor that Schrödinger co-designed. Schrödinger held a **~5.8% equity stake** (as of 12/31/25)
and is a co-inventor.

**Read it precisely:** the $2.3B is **upfront *plus* milestones**, not all cash on close. SDGR's
near-term inflow is ~5.8% of the *upfront* portion (undisclosed split) — meaningful but smaller than
"5.8% of $2.3B" would imply — *plus* its co-invention milestones/royalties as the molecule advances.
The deal needs HSR antitrust clearance; no firm close date is public. It is real, it is incremental
to guidance, and it's a clean validation of the FEP platform's economics — just don't model the
headline number as a lump-sum check.

## Instrument note — read this before thinking about structure

**Equity only. There are no LEAPS.** Verified against live options contract reference data: the
longest-dated SDGR contracts listed expire **December 18, 2026** (~6.5 months out). Expirations run
Jun / Jul / Sep / Nov / Dec 2026 — **no January 2027 and no January 2028 series exist.** The textbook
18-month LEAPS trade is not available on this name. Short-dated options exist but won't carry an
18-month thesis. Treat SDGR as an equity hold; do not reach for a LEAPS structure that isn't there.

LEAPS to cap an equity-only bet on a still-unprofitable name.
