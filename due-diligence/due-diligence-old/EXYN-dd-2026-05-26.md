## Section 2 — Counterparty Quality

### 2.1 — USSOCOM Vendor Loan Agreement (the headline)

**Per the [GlobeNewswire press release of 2026-05-19](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/19/3297393/0/en/Exyn-Defense-Subsidiary-Range-Advances-USSOCOM-Evaluation-Pathway-for-Autonomous-Systems.html):**

> "Range...executed a Vendor Loan Agreement with the United States Special
> Operations Command (USSOCOM) to establish a pathway for future adoption
> of Range's autonomy systems."

**What this actually is:**
A Vendor Loan Agreement (VLA) in the SOCOM context is a vendor-loaned
equipment arrangement that establishes a non-binding evaluation pathway.
The vendor (Exyn/Range) loans equipment to SOCOM for testing/evaluation;
SOCOM evaluates; no procurement is implied; no funds change hands; no
contract for production.

This is the **SOFWERX-tier evaluation pipeline** — many vendors have these,
they are NOT procurement. The pipeline from VLA → BAA award → OTA → production
contract is typically **18-36 months** and most VLAs do not convert.

### 5.8 — Hesai LiDAR / DJI supply chain — the structural deal-killer

**FY2025 NDAA Section 164 takes effect June 30, 2026.**

The Department of Defense is prohibited from "operating, procuring, or using"
certain LiDAR technology developed by Hesai or other companies on the Section
1260H list (companies linked to the Chinese military-industrial complex).
DJI components are already restricted under prior NDAA cycles and the
Securing Infrastructure from Adversaries Act of 2025.

Exyn's supply chain disclosure (per S-1, summarized in HDIN):
- ~15% of LiDAR sourcing from Hesai
- ~5% of structural parts from DJI
- Total Chinese supply chain exposure: > 20%

**Consequence:**
- After **June 30, 2026** (35 days from screening date), ANY DoD procurement
  of an Exyn-or-Range-built platform that uses Hesai LiDAR is BLOCKED unless
  the Secretary of Defense issues a national-interest waiver.
- The SOCOM VLA evaluation can continue (loaned equipment) but a production
  contract is blocked at the BoM level until Exyn requalifies on a
  non-Chinese LiDAR (Ouster, Hesai-replacement, Velodyne, etc.).
- LiDAR sensor requalification typically takes 12-18 months (sensor selection,
  driver/SDK integration, recalibration, regression testing, performance
  validation, customer requalification).
- Exyn's runway is 16-17 months. A LiDAR redesign consumes a substantial
  portion of that runway and there is no guarantee the autonomy stack will
  perform identically on a new sensor.

This is the **structural reason the defense pivot is at risk** — not because
the tech doesn't work, but because the platform cannot legally win DoD
procurement contracts until the sensor stack is requalified, and the company
cannot afford to do that without revenue or another raise.
