## Executive summary

ASP Isotopes is a Pretoria-based isotope enrichment company with a real, operating light-isotope business (oxygen-18, silicon-28, ytterbium-176, molybdenum-100) and a speculative HALEU thesis carried entirely by its subsidiary Quantum Leap Energy (QLE).

The "+475% YoY revenue growth" headline (FY2025 $23.85M vs FY2024 $4.14M) is a **factually correct but materially misleading** framing of the chokepoint thesis. Decomposed:
- ~$3.4M / $4.18M of Q1 2026 revenue (81%) is from **radiopharmaceutical doses** (PET Labs investment) — a medical-imaging-services business that has nothing to do with the HALEU chokepoint.
- ~$0.2M Q1 2026 (4.8%) is **nuclear fuels collaboration revenue** — pre-commercial R&D milestone recognition, not isotope sales.
- **HALEU revenue: $0.00.**
- The remainder is silicon-28 and Mo-100 sales — real light-isotope revenue but again not the nuclear-fuel thesis driving the rerate narrative.

The H10-N bellwether status (the May 11, 2026 8-K) is a **non-binding MOU** with an unnamed European nuclear technology company for HALEU supply starting 2028. This MOU is the third in a series of non-binding QLE arrangements:
1. Fermi America (Rick Perry's company) — Aug 14, 2025 — TX HyperGrid Campus JV, construction begins 2027, first reactor 2032
2. South African Nuclear Energy Corporation (Necsa) — Feb 20, 2026 — Pelindaba facility services contract
3. European nuclear reactor developer (unnamed) — May 11, 2026 — HALEU supply starting 2028

Every QLE HALEU relationship is non-binding. There is **no NRC license**, **no DOE LPO commitment**, **no funded DOE HALEU IDIQ award**, **no binding offtake from any utility/hyperscaler/SMR developer**. By the canonical H10-N criteria (NRC milestone OR DOE LPO OR binding utility/hyperscaler offtake with disclosed dollar value), **ASPI fails H10-N**. The MOUs satisfy H10-N FLAG, not PASS.

The technology itself is the harder concern. Klydon's Aerodynamic Separation Process (ASP) — the predecessor IP — was demonstrated commercially on oxygen-18 (2015) and silicon-28 (2018) in Pretoria. It has **never been tested on uranium hexafluoride (UF6)**. Quantum Enrichment (the laser-based extension) is in development for uranium-235 but is also untested on uranium. The Fuzzy Panda short report (Nov 26, 2024) interviewed a former Klydon scientist who stated the team "did not think it would work on Uranium." On Nov 27, 2024, in response to the report, CEO Paul Mann publicly admitted ASPI had **not actually enriched uranium**.

The securities fraud class action that followed (S.D.N.Y.) survived a motion to dismiss on **7 of 15 alleged misstatements**. The Court ruled actionable the statements that the technology was "fully prepared for deployment" and that the company had "used lasers to enrich" uranium. A tentative settlement has been reached.

ASPI's founding investor cohort included Jonathan Honig (brother of Barry Honig, lifetime-banned penny-stock fraudster) at a 6.2% IPO-era stake via Titan Multi-Strategy Fund. Fuzzy Panda flagged this as the SHAZ-pattern promotional infrastructure layer. Mann denies current involvement. The historical tie is documented in SEC filings.

Insider behavior since the rerate has been **net-selling at peaks**:
- Most recent open-market purchase: **2024-07-15** (~22 months ago)
- Net 90-day insider net_value: -$419K but mostly 10b5-1 mechanical
- Net 365-day insider net_value: **-$6.53M (32 sells / 22 "buys" that are entirely RSU/option grants and exercises)**
- COO Robert Ainscow sold 22,500 shares at $13.60 (weighted average) on Oct 14, 2025 — selling **at the 52-week high**
- CEO Paul Mann sold ~162K shares twice (Nov 25, 2025 + Mar 2, 2026), with cumulative 12-month sales >$9M
- Directors Duncan Moore, Todd Wider, Michael Gorley each disposed $500K-$700K Aug-Sept 2025

Cash position is the one genuine strength: $207M cash + $83M short-term investments = **~$290M total liquidity** at March 31, 2026. Combined with ongoing operating burn of ~$22M/quarter, runway is ~13 quarters before next required raise. There is **no going-concern qualification** in the FY2025 audit (EisnerAmper LLP) — but the company raised ~$381.9M in 2025 alone, took on a TerraPower vendor loan at 10% OID + 10% fixed interest (effective ~20% capital cost), and has expanded share count to 125.9M (from ~50-60M pre-2024).

**The Renergen acquisition (regulatory approvals Q3 2025; $750M project finance from DFC $500M + Standard Bank SA $250M)** transformed ASPI into a partial LNG/helium operator on top of the isotope business. The 16% dilution to Renergen holders and the addition of South African LNG sales explain part of the +475% revenue growth (Q1 2026 mentions "new LNG sales"). The Renergen deal makes the parent more diversified but adds **two new businesses unrelated to the chokepoint thesis** (helium and LNG to the South African energy market).

The competitive position is the killing blow. **Centrus Energy (LEU) — the canonical Phase 3 winner already documented in CANDIDATE_UNIVERSE.md** — has:
- A **$900M DOE task order** announced Jan 5, 2026 for commercial-scale HALEU expansion at Piketon, OH
- **1+ metric ton of HALEU UF6 actually produced and delivered to DOE in 2025**
- **$2.3B commercial LEU backlog**
- 12 additional metric tons of HALEU capacity build-out
- NNSA **sole-source authority** for certain uranium enrichment activities
- 21% short interest at the 2024 low → multi-billion rerate

LEU is the H10-N-PASS calibration case for the entire nuclear lens. ASPI is at the MOU stage with European counterparties whose names are not disclosed, while LEU is delivering HALEU to the federal government against a $900M DOE task order. The race is not close. The DOE has chosen its U.S. domestic HALEU supplier, and it is LEU.
