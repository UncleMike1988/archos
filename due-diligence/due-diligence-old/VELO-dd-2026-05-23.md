| **2025** | **Not in top 3 customers** | [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1825079/000119312526134997/velo-20251231.htm) |

Per the FY2025 [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1825079/000119312526134997/velo-20251231.htm) (verbatim):

> "For the years ended December 31, 2025 and 2024, sales to the top
> three customers accounted for 33.2% and 47.0% of our revenue,
> respectively. **For the year ended December 31, 2025, all three
> customers were different from the top three customers for the
> comparable period in 2024.**"

This is the smoking gun: the top three customers in 2025 are entirely
different from 2024 — meaning SpaceX (the 23% customer in 2024) is no
longer in the top 3 in 2025.

#### The September 2024 IP licensing deal:
Per the FY2025 [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1825079/000119312526134997/velo-20251231.htm) MD&A (verbatim):

> "The decrease in other revenue was attributed to a licensing agreement
> with SpaceX in September 2024, where **SpaceX obtained a non-exclusive
> license to certain Velo3D patents and technologies for its internal
> uses only**."

The September 2024 8-K detail (per SEC full-text search snippet): SpaceX
paid Velo3D approximately $8M ($5M license + $3M engineering support)
for a non-exclusive perpetual license to use Velo3D's metal AM technology
**internally**. After this, SpaceX has reportedly installed ~30 metal AM
systems at its Boca Chica facility for in-house Raptor 3 production.

This is the classic "licensee replaces supplier" pattern — Velo3D
received a lump-sum settlement; SpaceX now produces in-house.

#### Anduril and DoW contracts (real, but smaller than thesis implies):
- **Anduril**: Named as a current customer by CEO Jeldi in December 2025
  (per Benzinga reporting); described in Q1 2026 earnings materials as
  "multiple Anduril programs" — actual revenue contribution undisclosed.
- **$32.6M DoW (Department of War) contract**: Announced for AM
  qualification of slow-traditional metal parts. This appears to be a
  multi-year contract; not all $32.6M lands in 2026.
- **U.S. Army Ground Vehicle Systems Center**: Procurement disclosed.
- **NDAA compliance angle**: Velo3D is positioned as "the only company
  with domestically created Laser Powder-Bed Fusion technology" compliant
  with NDAA's foreign-equipment prohibitions. This is a real moat in
  defense procurement, although competitors (3D Systems, Optomec, GE
  Additive) also have domestic offerings.

#### Counterparty viability:
- **SpaceX** is obviously solvent and able to pay; but the relationship
  is now structured as a one-time IP license, not ongoing supply.
- **Anduril** is well-capitalized ($30B+ valuation per recent funding);
  good counterparty.
- **U.S. Department of War / Army**: Best possible counterparty (USG).

### 2.4 Strategic logic of counterparties
- The strategic logic of defense customers buying Velo3D printers
  (NDAA-compliant domestic supplier) is sound.
- The strategic logic of SpaceX's relationship is now historical — SpaceX
  has captured the IP and built in-house capacity.

**Section 2 score: FLAG**

Defense pivot to Anduril and DoW is genuine and the strategic logic is
sound. However, the SpaceX thesis is broken: SpaceX is no longer a
top-3 customer and the relationship is structured as a one-time IP
license enabling SpaceX's in-house production. The counterparty narrative
in press releases ("strategic partner to innovators such as SpaceX,
Honeywell, Honda, Chromalloy, and Lam Research") continues to lead with
SpaceX but the financial reality has diverged. Defense replacement is
real but unverified for the size needed to justify $687M market cap.

### Historical precedent
