## Section 2 — Counterparty Quality

**Score: FLAG**

### 2.1 Customer concentration (the CRITICAL question)
Per the [FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/830616/000110465926015820/0001104659-26-015820-index.htm) Risk Factors:
> "Our top two customers accounted for approximately 84.5% of our net sales for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, compared to 88.4% the prior year."
> "Our largest customer represented approximately 66% of total revenue in 2025 and 74% in 2024."

Per the [Q1 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/830616/000110465926053487/0001104659-26-053487-index.htm) Revenue Note:
> "The top two customers represented 87% and 80% of total revenue for the three months ended March 31, 2026 and 2025, respectively."

**CRITICAL OBSERVATION:** Customer concentration WORSENED in the +133% inflection. Top-2 went from 80% (Q1 2025) to 87% (Q1 2026). If the inflection were being driven by NEW chokepoint customers (CHIPS Act fabs, defense primes, etc.), top-2 concentration should be DECREASING. It's increasing. **This is the strongest single piece of evidence that the +133% is being driven by EXISTING top customers ordering more, not by new structural demand.**

Per the 10-K Risk Factors:
> "Although they have been major customers of the Company for more than twenty years, we do not have written agreements with these customers that require any minimum purchase obligations..."

**Customers are NOT identified by name** in any filing. They are 20+ year relationships on PO basis with no LTA / minimum purchase obligations. They could be: a major display manufacturer, a glass coatings OEM, a solar panel maker, a defense prime — the filings don't say.

**Single-point-of-failure risk:** YES. 66% of total revenue from one customer. If that customer reduces orders or in-sources, SCIA is structurally damaged. Mitigating: this is a 20+ year relationship, not a single-deal newcomer like ESDS was to SHAZ.

### 2.2 Counterparty payment capacity

**Counterparty payment capacity is empirically demonstrated** (customers are pre-paying), even though we don't know the customers' names. This is materially better than the SHAZ ESDS case where a $40M counterparty had to pay $250M/year.

## BONUS — Cyclical vs Structural Analysis

**Verdict: PRIMARILY CYCLICAL with two structural overlays (new product introductions; emerging defense)**

### CYCLICAL EVIDENCE (dominant)
1. **Customer concentration is WORSENING** (80% top-2 Q1 2025 → 87% Q1 2026). A structural new-customer story would show DECREASING concentration. This is the strongest single piece of evidence.

3. **Gross margin compressed in Q1 2026** (24.9% vs 30.6% Q1 2025). A supply-constrained chokepoint supplier would expand margins on tight supply. SCI is passing through raw material costs at lower-margin product mix — that's volume-driven cyclical recovery, not pricing-power chokepoint.

6. **International revenue is ~0.4%** — almost entirely domestic. This is consistent with established U.S. customer relationships, not new global chokepoint demand.

### STRUCTURAL OVERLAYS (real but not dominant)
1. **New rotatable target + conductive ITO products** launched in H2 2025 are explicitly cited as contributors to Q1 2026 growth. These are NEW capabilities that didn't exist in the prior cycle.
2. **New customers** are mentioned in the Q1 10-Q MD&A: "Sales products introduced during the second half of 2025 and sales to new customers primarily contributed to the year-over-year increase." But these new customers are NOT enough to reduce top-2 concentration (87%).

4. **3-meter rotatable targets** are a new product category — useful for Gen 10.5 display fabs and large-format architectural glass / solar. This is a real product innovation.

### THE ANSWER

This is **a cyclical recovery (~70-75% of the story) with structural overlays (~25-30% of the story)**.

The cyclical case: existing display/solar/glass customers ordering more as their end-markets recover from the 2024 trough. Backlog and customer deposits indicate the cycle continues through 2026. Revenue probably reaches $30-35M for FY2026.

The structural case: new rotatable target + ITO products, plus the emerging defense boron carbide segment, plus the ColdFIRE® tactical product, could add 15-25% incremental annual growth on top of the cyclical recovery if Crisler-era defense penetration succeeds.
