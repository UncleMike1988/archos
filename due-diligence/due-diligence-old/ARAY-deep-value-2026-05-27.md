# QUESTION 1 — WHAT IS THE CNNC JV ACTUALLY WORTH?

## 1.1 The JV — name, structure, accounting

- **Legal name:** CNNC Accuray (Tianjin) Medical Technology Co., Ltd.
- **Formed:** Agreement signed January 2019; operationally established July 2019
- **Ownership:** **49% Accuray Asia Limited / 51% CNNC High Energy Equipment
  (Tianjin) Co., Ltd.** (a wholly-owned subsidiary of China Isotope &
  Radiation Corporation / "CIRC")
- **CIRC parent:** CIRC is 73.83% owned by China National Nuclear
  Corporation (CNNC), a state-owned nuclear conglomerate
- **JV CEO:** Roger Cao (former Accuray executive — installed at JV launch)
- **Accounting:** Equity method on ARAY's books — *one-quarter lag* due to
  timing of JV financial records. (March 31, 2026 ARAY balance sheet
  reflects December 31, 2025 JV data.)
- **What it does:** Manufactures the **locally-branded "Made in China"
  Tomo C** radiation therapy system in the **Class B license category**
  (lower-tier hospitals). Also distributes ARAY's Radixact and CyberKnife
  systems (Radixact SynC and CyberKnife S7 received NMPA approval in
  January 2025).
- Source: [10-Q Q3 FY26 Note 12](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1138723/000143774926015199/aray20260331_10q.htm)
  and [FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1138723/000119312525191242/aray-20250630.htm)

## 1.5 CIRC (the 51% partner) — what is its scale?

**The mistaken FinTwit framing — "CNNC does tens of billions, so ARAY's

and is contracting fast.

## 1.6 The China-state risk factor that should change everyone's
       valuation framework

ARAY's own FY25 10-K Item 1A risk factors state:

> "CNNC is also on the United States Department of Defense's list of
> Chinese Military Companies operating directly or indirectly in the
> United States under section 1260H of the National Defense
> Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021. This designation may result
> in negative publicity for us and the JV. Further regulatory changes
> adding CNNC or our JV partner to additional lists or export and
> sanctions related restricted or prohibited parties or further
> controls on entities viewed as connected to the Chinese military
> could impact the JV."

ARAY also flags the **DOJ Sensitive Personal Data Transfer
Limitations Rule** as a potential restriction on transferring
medical-imagery data to the JV. CNNC being a designated Chinese
Military Company means:

- Any potential acquirer of ARAY faces CFIUS review of the JV stake
- US institutional capital that excludes 1260H entities cannot
  cleanly own ARAY (the JV revenue is "downstream" of a CMC parent)
- Risk of additional sanctions/export controls during ongoing
  US-China tension
- A direct echo of the **Hesai NDAA cliff that killed EXYN's
  investability**. The mechanism is identical: a foreign state-linked
  counterparty whose status can be further downgraded by US
  regulatory action without the company's control.

- ARAY paid an additional $2.4M in fees to TCW

- Translation: ARAY could not have met covenants going into Q2
  FY26; TCW pushed the test out and took fees + more warrants

ARAY is in compliance with covenants as of March 31, 2026 (per Note 8),
but the **next live test is December 31, 2026** — and the company
is reporting declining revenue, withdrawn FY26 guidance, and
geopolitical headwinds going into that test.

# QUESTION 3 — INSTALLED BASE & COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

## 3.1 Installed base

Accuray does not disclose an exact current global system count in
its public filings. Triangulating from history:

- 2011: ~240 CyberKnife + 342 TomoTherapy = ~580 systems
- 2015: >300 CyberKnife + >500 TomoTherapy = >800 systems
- More recent press releases reference "more than 700 systems
  worldwide" (combined platforms)

Given fiscal 2026's ~$220M annualized service revenue and typical
service plans of $250-400K per system annually, **the implied
installed base is ~600-900 systems**. That is the right order of
magnitude. The exact number is not material to this analysis —
what matters is:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Service revenue (9M FY26) | $169.1M (up +3% YoY) |
| Service revenue (annualized) | ~$225M |
| Estimated systems | ~700 |
| Implied annual service per system | ~$320K |
| Service gross margin (implied) | ~29% (better than products) |
| Service revenue stickiness | Multi-year contracts; growing on installed base growth |

**Services are growing even as product revenue collapses (-21% YTD).**
This is the actual asset. ARAY's installed base is its only durable
moat — once a hospital adopts a CyberKnife or TomoTherapy system, the
switching cost is real (capital expense already sunk; staff trained;
treatment protocols built around it).

## 3.4 The Siemens-Varian comp — what would ARAY be worth in an
       acquisition?

Siemens Healthineers paid 5.0x revenue for Varian in 2020, at a 42%
premium to 30-day VWAP. Varian was the market leader, growing,
profitable, and clean. **ARAY is the opposite on every dimension** —
distressed, contracting, loss-making, debt-encumbered, China-tied.

Reasonable acquisition value bracketing for ARAY:

| Scenario | EV/Revenue | EV | Less debt | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distressed roll-up | 0.25x | $107M | $179M | **negative** | — |
| Slow turnaround comp | 0.50x | $214M | $179M | $35M | $0.22 (FD) |
| Elekta-style comp (no premium) | 1.0x | $429M | $179M | $250M | $1.60 (FD) |
| Elekta + 30% control premium | 1.3x | $558M | $179M | $379M | $2.43 (FD) |
| Varian-style comp (unrealistic) | 5.0x | $2,145M | $179M | $1,966M | $12.58 (FD) |

The Elekta-style comp is the right reference point. **At 1.0-1.3x
revenue an acquirer could pay $1.60-$2.43 per fully-diluted share**
— 4-7x today's $0.37 price — and still be paying a distressed-asset
multiple. BUT this requires an acquirer willing to:

- Assume $179M of TCW-secured debt
- Inherit the CNNC NDAA-CMC counterparty relationship
- Manage through the China revenue collapse
- Absorb 37M warrants priced at $0.01-$1.68

**Who could acquire?** The list is short:
- **Elekta** — natural strategic; but balance sheet not strong
  enough to absorb $179M of debt cleanly; CFIUS-friendly (Swedish)
- **A Chinese state-linked acquirer (e.g., CIRC itself)** — already
  knows the JV, could buy out the 49% and the parent
  simultaneously; politically opposite outcome (delisting, CFIUS
  block likely)
- **A PE-led take-private**: TCW already controls the cap stack —
  could partner with PE to take the company private at a small
  premium, fold the JV into a separate vehicle, run the
  installed-base service business as a cash flow asset
- **A Japanese acquirer (e.g., Hitachi)** — declining likelihood;
  ARAY's Japan revenue is also contracting

# BONUS — RADIATION THERAPY MARKET LANDSCAPE

## Market size and structure

- Global radiation therapy equipment market: ~$8-10B annually
- Growth: ~5-7% CAGR (slow, replacement-driven, EM expansion)
- Structure: duopoly Varian (Siemens Healthineers, ~50% share) +
  Elekta (~30% share); Accuray + others fill the long tail
- Service & software an increasing share of revenue (sticky moat)

## Adaptive radiation therapy — is Stellar real?

Adaptive RT is a real clinical trend (treatments re-planned daily
based on patient anatomy changes), led by:
- **Varian Ethos** (the main competitor in adaptive)
- **Elekta Unity** (MRI-guided adaptive)
- **Accuray Stellar** (adaptive on the TomoTherapy / Radixact base)

The technology differentiation between platforms is real but not
overwhelming. Stellar is positioned as a software-led upgrade to
existing Radixact installs — a credible defensive moat for the

# OVERALL VERDICT

## THE THREE QUESTIONS THAT DETERMINE EVERYTHING

### 1. CNNC JV — what is it worth? Is 49% of it worth more than $45M?

**No.** The JV's actual financials, on a one-quarter lag, show
revenue collapsing 46% over 9 months ($146M → $78M), net income
shrinking 66%, and stockholders equity of just $38.9M total (ARAY's
49% = $19.1M proportional share). A realistic mid-range valuation
of ARAY's stake is **$25-50M — roughly equal to, not multiples of,
ARAY's current $44M market cap**. The hidden-asset thesis fails the
magnitude test.

The JV is also **structurally compromised**: CNNC (the
grandparent) is on the DoD Section 1260H Chinese Military Companies
list. This is the same regulatory mechanism that killed EXYN's
investability via the Hesai NDAA cliff. Any acquirer faces CFIUS;
any compliance-sensitive US fund cannot cleanly own it.

### 2. Cash runway — can ARAY survive 12-18 months at current burn?

**Probably yes, just barely.** $38.1M cash + $18.25M delayed draw
(already deployed to pay the June 1 conv note) + IEEPA tariff cash
of $6.6M + $10-12M annualized restructuring savings + revolver
capacity. Quarterly burn ~$6-7M all-in. Runway extends through the
**Dec 31, 2026 covenant test**, which is the real cliff.

The structural debt situation is grim: **24.5% effective rate on
the term loan, ~$179M face debt, TCW first-lien on substantially
all assets, 37.4M warrant overhang (31% dilution), covenant compliance
already deferred once at a cost of $2.4M in fees**. ARAY is
operationally alive but financially captured by TCW.

### 3. Installed base — is this an acquirable asset at these prices?

**Yes, in theory. No, in practice — at least not at a premium.**
~700 systems generating ~$225M sticky service revenue + an
underlying $200M+ products business = a genuine medical-device
asset. Comparing to Elekta (1.1x P/S) would imply $400-500M EV /
~$1.60-2.40 per fully-diluted share. But:

- TCW has first-priority liens on substantially all assets
- $179M of debt sits above the equity
- The China NDAA-CMC overhang scares strategic acquirers
- A take-private led by TCW + PE is the cleanest acquisition path
  and would by definition be at a low premium (or no premium)

## THE STRATEGIC QUESTION

**At $44M market cap with $430M revenue and a CNNC JV stake, is ARAY
a deep value asymmetric setup or a value trap heading toward
dilution / restructuring / delisting?**

**This is closer to a value trap than to an asymmetric setup.** The
asymmetry the user intuited *almost* exists — ARAY truly is trading
at a fraction of its replacement value and at a fraction of any
peer's revenue multiple. But that asymmetry is **almost entirely
captured by TCW**, not the equity. Every dollar of installed-base
value flows through:

1. The $179M secured TCW debt (24.5% effective rate, growing via PIK)
2. The 37.4M warrant overhang (31% dilution at $0.01-$1.68 strikes)
3. The Dec 31, 2026 covenant cliff

Equity holders sit in the residual position with limited ability to
force a premium outcome. The most likely two paths from here:

- **Path A (60% probability):** ARAY executes the Transformation Plan,
  meets Dec 2026 covenants, gradually deleverages, stock recovers
  to $1.00-$2.00 as the China revenue collapse stops and the US
  Americas/EIMEA growth bleeds through. *Upside scenario: 3-5x
  from $0.37 over 24-36 months.*
- **Path B (40% probability):** Covenant breach in Dec 2026 or
  earlier → TCW takes over via debt-for-equity restructuring → existing
  equity diluted 90%+ or wiped out. *Downside: -80 to -100%.*

The Path A scenario could produce a real return. But the *expected*
value is much closer to -10% to 0% than to +200% — meaning **the
median outcome is mediocre, with skew in both directions**.
